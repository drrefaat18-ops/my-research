The aim of this study was to investigate the effects of palmitoylethanolamide (PEA), an endogenous fatty

induced uveitis (EIU). EIU was induced in male rats by a single footpad injection of 200 μg
lipopolysaccharide (LPS). PEA was administered intraperitoneally at 1 h before and 7 h after injection
of LPS. Another group of animals was treated with vehicle. Dexamethasone (DEX) was administered as a
positive control. Rats were sacriﬁced 16 h after injection and the eyes tissues were collected for
histology, immunohistochemical and western blot analyses. The histological evaluation of the iris–ciliary
body showed an increase of neutrophilic inﬁltration and nuclear modiﬁcation of vessel of endothelial
cells. PEA treatment decreased the inﬂammatory cell inﬁltration and improved histological damage of
eye tissues. In addition, PEA treatment reduced pro-inﬂammatory tumor necrosis factor (TNF-α) levels,
protein extravasion and lipid peroxidation. Immunohistochemical analysis for intracellular adhesion
molecule (ICAM)-1 and nitrotyrosine showed a positive staining from LPS-injected rats. The degree of
staining for ICAM-1 and nitrotyrosine was signiﬁcantly reduced in eye sections from LPS-injected rats
treated with PEA. In addition, an increase of inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) and nuclear factor
(NF-κB) was also evaluated in inﬂammed ocular tissues by western blot. PEA strongly inhibited iNOS
expression and nuclear NF-κB translocation. Thus, in this study we demonstrated that PEA reduces the
degree of ocular inﬂammation in a rat model of EIU.

1. Introduction
Anterior uveitis is an inﬂammation of the iris and parts of the
ciliary body. The main therapies for uveitis are corticosteroids but
some patients are still resistant to them (Bucolo et al., 2003).
Endotoxin induced uveitis (EIU) is a useful animal model for acute
anterior uveitis in humans but the cellular mechanisms activated by
lipopolysaccharide (LPS) injection are still not clear (Bucolo et al.,
2003). Activation of signaling pathways and alterations of the expres-
sion of various inﬂammatory genes are common characteristics in
ocular inﬂamed tissues (Kalariya et al., 2010; Kim and Moudgil, 2008).
Activation of nuclear factor (NF-κB) is known to mediate inﬂammatory
diseases, including uveitis (Kalariya et al., 2010). Because of uveal
inﬂammation, the levels of pro-inﬂammatory cytokines, chemokines
and adhesion molecules in ocular tissues increase signiﬁcantly (Bucolo
et al., 2003). Several studies demonstrated that injection of different
cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, interleukin (IL)-1, IL-
6, IL-8, into rat eyes can cause acute uveitis (Chiou et al., 2000;
Lemaitre et al., 2001). In addition, high levels of TNF-α were also found
in the aqueous humor after LPS injection (Smith et al., 1998). In that
regard, it was shown an increased intercellular adhesion molecule 1
(ICAM-1) expression on the vascular endothelium of the rat ciliary
body during EIU (Bucolo et al., 2003). Inﬂammation has a strong
correlation with the oxidative stress. Among free radicals, nitric oxide
(NO) has the potential to mediate the vascular dysfunction associated
with EIU (Goureau et al.,1995). Previous studies demonstrated that the
NO-related injury may be due to peroxynitrite formation (Szabo,
1996).
N-palmitoylethanolamide (PEA) is an endogenous fatty acid amide
belonging to the family of the N-acylethanolamines (NAEs). PEA has an
important analgesic, anti-inﬂammatory and neuroprotective action,
acting at different molecular targets such as immune cells (Hoareau
et al., 2006). Several studies demonstrated that the endogenous tissue

2. Materials and methods
2.1. Animals
Male Lewis rats (160–180 gr) were obtained from Harlan Nossan,
Italy. All the animals were treated according to the ARVO Statement
for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision Research.
2.2. EIU induction, protein concentration and TNF-α levels
Uveitis was induced in rats by foot injection of 200 μg LPS
(Salmonella minnesota). PEA (10 mg/kg, 10% ethanol) was injected
intraperitoneally 1 h before and 7 h after LPS injection. Control
group received LPS injection and vehicle (ethanol), 1 h before and
7 h after LPS injection. DEX (1 mg/kg) was also administered as a
positive control 1 h before and 7 h after LPS injection (Mangano
et al., 2008). Sham animals were not injected by LPS but only
treated with vehicle or PEA or DEX. Uveitis in each eye was graded
using a scoring system as previously reported (Behar-Cohen et al.,
1998): 0¼no inﬂammatory reaction; 1¼discrete dilatation of the
iris and conjunctival vessels; 2¼moderate dilatation of the iris and
conjunctival vessels; 3¼intense iridal hyperemia with ﬂare in
anterior chamber; 4¼same clinical signs as 3 plus the presence of
ﬁbrinous exudate in the pupillary area with intense ﬂare in the
anterior chamber. Sixteen hours after uveitis induction, the rats
were killed and aqueous humor and iris–ciliary body of right and
left eyes were collected. Aqueous humor samples were pooled and
the protein concentration was detected by the Bradford assay. In a
separate set of animals, TNF-α levels were measured in aqueous
humor at 16 h after LPS injection. The assay was carried out by
using
a
ELISA,
commercial
kit
(Calbiochem-Novabiochem
Corporation, USA).
2.3. Histological examination
Sixteen hours after uveitis induction, eye tissues were collected
and ﬁxed in a solution 0.1% glutareldehyde (25%) and 4% paraf-
ormaldehyde for 24 h at room temperature, dehydrated by graded
ethanol and embedded in paraplast (Sherwood Medical, Mahwh,
NJ, USA). The ocular sections (7 μm) were deparafﬁnized with
xylene, stained with May-Grunwald–Giemsa and studied using
light microscopy (Dialux 22 Leitz).
2.4. Evaluation of inﬁltrating cells and myeloperoxidase activity
The number of neutrophils was counted on histological sections of
the iris–ciliary body by an expert unaware of the treatment groups.
For each animal, the number of neutrophils is the mean of three serial
sections. Myeloperoxidase (MPO) activity, a marker of neutrophil
inﬁltration, was measured as previously indicated (Bucolo et al.,
2003). Brieﬂy, 16 h after LPS, eye tissues were weighed and homo-
genized in a solution containing 0.5% (wt/vol) hexadecyltrimethylam-
monium bromide dissolved in 10 mmol/l potassium phosphate buffer
(pH 7.4) and centrifuged for 30 min at 20,000g at 4 1C. An aliquot of
supernatant was added to a reaction mixture containing 1.6 mmol/l
tetramethylbenzidine and 0.1 mmol/l hydrogen peroxide (H2O2). The
rate of change in absorbance was detected spectrophotometrically at
650 nm. MPO activity was deﬁned as the quantity of enzyme required
degrading 1 mmol of H2O2 at 37 1C and was expressed in U/g
wet tissue.
2.5. Malondialdehyde (MDA) measurement
Levels of MDA into the eyes tissues were determined as an
index of lipid peroxidation (Talero et al., 2012). Brieﬂy, eye tissues
were weighed and homogenized in a 1.15% (wt/vol) KCl solution.
100 μl aliquots of homogenate were added to a reaction mixture
containing 200 μl 8.1% (wt/vol) lauryl sulfate, 1.5 ml 20% (vol/vol)
acetic acid (pH 3.5), 1.5 ml 0.8% (wt/vol) thiobarbituric acid, and
700 μl distilled water. Samples were then boiled for 1 h at 95 1C
and centrifuged at 3000g for 10 min. The absorbance of the
supernatant was measured spectrophotometrically at 532 nm.
MDA levels were expressed as mM /100 mg wet tissue.
2.6. Immunohistochemical localization of ICAM-1 and nitrotyrosine
After 16 h from the injection of LPS, iris–ciliary bodies were
collected and ﬁxed in 10% (w/v) PBS-buffered formaldehyde and
7 μm sections were prepared from parafﬁn embedded tissues.
After deparafﬁnization, endogenous peroxidase was quenched
with 0.3% (v/v) hydrogen peroxide in 60% (v/v) methanol for
30 min. The sections were permeabilized with 0.1% (w/v) Triton
X-100 in PBS for 20 min. Non-speciﬁc adsorption was minimized
by incubating the sections in 2% (v/v) normal goat serum in PBS for
20 min. Endogenous biotin or avidin binding sites were blocked by
sequential incubation for 15 min with biotin and avidin (Vector
Laboratories, Burlingame, CA), respectively. Tissue sections were
incubated overnight with: (1) puriﬁed hamster anti-mouse ICAM-
1 (CD54) (AbD Serotec, MCA1371Z, 1:500 in PBS, w/v) or (2) anti-
nitrotyrosine antibody (Millipore, 06-284). Sections were washed
with PBS and incubated with secondary antibody. Speciﬁc labeling
was determined with a biotin-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG and
avidin–biotin peroxidase complex (Vector Laboratories, Burlin-
game, CA). The counter stain was developed with diaminobenzi-
dine (brown color) and nuclear fast red (red background). Positive
staining (brown color) was found in the sections, indicating that
the immunoreactions were positive. To verify the binding speciﬁ-
city for ICAM-1 and nitrotyrosine some sections were also incu-
bated with primary antibody only (no secondary antibody) or with
secondary antibody only (no primary antibody). In these situa-
tions, no positive staining was found in the sections. Immunohis-
tochemistry photographs were assessed by densitometry by using
Optilab Graftek software on a Macintosh personal computer.

3. Results
3.1. Histopathology of EIU and clinical score
Histological analysis of iris ciliary-body tissues from LPS injected
animals showed signs of severe uveitis with a strong neutrophil
inﬁltration (Fig. 1B and B1). In epithelial cells from LPS injected rats
was observed reduced cytoplasm and visible nucleus (Fig. 1B and B1).
In PEA-treated animals, the degree of inﬂammation was signiﬁcantly
reduced (Fig. 1C and C1) with similar characteristics to the sham-
treated rats (Fig. 1A and A1).
The number of neutrophils was also signiﬁcantly decreased in the
group treated with PEA (Fig. 2B) as well as the MPO activity on iris–
ciliary body samples (Fig. 2C). In addition, 16 h after LPS injection, the
clinical inﬂammatory score, was signiﬁcantly reduced in PEA group
compared with controls (Fig. 2A). The effect of PEA was comparable
with that of the positive control group receiving DEX (Fig. 1D and D1;
Fig. 2A–C).
3.2. MDA, protein and TNF-α levels
16 h after LPS injection, increased protein levels were observed in
the aqueous humor from LPS injected rats (Fig. 3A). PEA treatment
signiﬁcantly decreased the leakage of proteins in the eye tissue
(Fig. 3A). As shown in Fig. 3B the levels of TNF-α in the aqueous
humor were signiﬁcantly reduced in PEA-treated rats compared to the
vehicle group. In addition, the levels of lipid peroxidation, measured
by MDA levels in ocular tissues, were decreased in PEA-treated rats
compared to vehicle group (Fig. 3C). The effect was comparable with
that observed with DEX (Fig. 3A–C).
3.3. Effects of PEA on adhesion molecule expression
The results obtained from histological analysis are correlated with
immunohistochemical staining for adhesion molecule ICAM-1. The
sections from LPS injected rats showed intense positive staining for
ICAM-1 (Fig. 4 B and see E). On the contrary, reduced positive ICAM-1
staining was observed in the eyes samples of PEA-treated rats (Fig. 4C
and E). A similar effect was also found in the eyes sample treated with
DEX (Fig. 4D and E). No staining was observed in sham animals
(Fig. 4A and E).
3.4. Effects of PEA on nitrotyrosine and iNOS expression
No positive staining was observed for nitrotyrosine in the iris–
ciliary body from sham-treated rats (Fig. 5A and E). Immunohisto-
chemical analysis of tissue sections obtained from LPS injected rats
showed positive staining for nitrotyrosine (Fig. 5B and E). Moreover, a
reduced positive nitrotyrosine staining was found in the eye sections
of mice treated with PEA (Fig. 5C and E) or positive control DEX
(Fig. 5D and E) compared to vehicle group In addition, we also
evaluated by western blot the expression of iNOS. A signiﬁcant
increase of iNOS expression 16 h after LPS injection was also detected
in iris–ciliary body tissues obtained from mice subjected to LPS-
induced uveitis (Fig. 6C). PEA treatment signiﬁcantly attenuated iNOS
expression (Fig. 6C).

vessels to intense iridal hyperemia with ﬂare in the anterior
chamber, along with ﬁbrinous exudate in the pupillary area. These

clinical signs are accompanied by augmented content of proteins
and inﬂammatory mediators such as interleukin-6, TNF-α and NO
in the aqueous humor (Bucolo et al., 2003; Jin et al., 2006; Suzuki
et al., 2006). A spontaneous regression of clinical, histological and
seroimmunological signs of EIU can be observed starting 16–24 h
after LPS challenge, with marked amelioration being observable
within 72 h after induction of the disease (Medeiros et al., 2008).
In that regard, the aim of this study was to investigate whether
PEA, a member of the fatty acid ethanolamine family, could
modulate the inﬂammatory events leading to EIU after injection
of LPS. In particular, we propose the following cycle: LPS induced
eye inﬂammation – reactive oxygen species production – NF-κB
pathway modulation – proinﬂammatory cytokines and NO release
– endothelial injury – neutrophil inﬁltration – eye tissue damage.
It has previously been shown that speciﬁc blockade of NF-κB, TNF-
α and NO exerts beneﬁcial effects on the course of EIU (Kitamei
et al., 2006; Suzuki et al., 2006). Thus, because of its important
pharmacological properties, PEA treatment, by the interaction
with this timeline of events could reduce the eye inﬂammation
induced by LPS injection in a rat model of uveitis.
Several studies have demonstrated that PEA has important
analgesic, anti-inﬂammatory and neuroprotective activities (Ahmad
et al., 2012; Esposito et al., 2012). A recent study demonstrated that
ultramicronized PEA attenuated the renal dysfunction and injury
associated with ischemia reperfusion (IR) (Di Paola et al., 2012a).
Another study in a compression model of spinal cord injury (Esposito
et al., 2011) showed that ultramicronized PEA treatment decreases
mast cell (MC) inﬁltration and activation, which occurs not only
during inﬂammation, but also in neuropathic hyperalgesia. Previous
works (Smith et al., 1998) demonstrated that the neutrophil inﬁltra-
tion has a key role in the pathogenesis of EIU (Smith et al., 1998).
During inﬂammatory conditions, adhesion molecules such as ICAM-1
are expressed on endothelium (Roebuck, 1999; Whitcup et al., 1995)
and are activated by different cytokines. In that regard, PEA treat-
ment decreased the levels of TNF-α and ICAM-1 expression in
various models of inﬂammation (Di Paola et al., 2012a, 2012b). In
the present study we also observed a signiﬁcant reduction of TNF-α
levels in the aqueous humor and ICAM-1 expression in iris–ciliary
body sections obtained from rats injected with LPS and treated with
PEA. It is possible that the diminished expression of ICAM-1 in the
iris ciliary bodies during development of EIU may be attributed to
drug-induced downregulation of TNF-α production. In addition, we
showed that PEA decreased the levels of proteins in the aqueous
humor and neutrophil inﬁltration, measured by MPO activity, in eyes
samples obtained from EIU-subjected rats treated with PEA. Thus, by
inhibiting TNF-α induction and intraocular expression of ICAM-1,
PEA may have prevented inﬁltration of the eyes with inﬂammatory
cells and, hence, local production of pro-inﬂammatory mediators.
Oxidative stress is also suggested to induce eye inﬂammation
(Hollyﬁeld et al., 2008; Sellak et al., 1994). Oxidative biomarkers are
elevated in EIU (Satici et al., 2003) suggesting that inﬂammation
and oxidative stress together contribute to its pathogenesis (Kubota
et al., 2009). ROS can activate transcription factors such as NF-κB
which plays a central role in inﬂammation (Kitamei et al., 2006;
Wang et al., 2002). Under normal conditions, NF-κB is sequestered
in the cytosol bound to the inhibitor protein (I)κB-α. Exposure of
cells to LPS activates the phosphorylation and degradation of IκB-α.
Once IκB-α dissociates from the complex, NF-κB can translocate
into the nucleus and can activate inﬂammatory genes transcription.
Our data showed that PEA reduced the translocation of NF-κB p65
in nucleus and the degradation of IκB-α in inﬂammed eyes tissues.
These results are also in agreement with a previous study in which
central administration of PEA reduces hyperalgesia via inhibition of
nuclear NF-κB pathway in dorsal root ganglia (D'Agostino et al.,
2009). Thus, in our work the reduction of TNF-α levels, adhesion
molecule (ICAM-1) and neutrophil inﬁltration could be also related
to the the inhibitory effect of PEA on NF-κB pathway. Moreover, the
exact mechanisms by which PEA suppresses NF-κB activation are
not known; in several inﬂammatory experimental models, it was
related to activation of PPAR-α receptor (Di Paola et al., 2012a) but
some other pathways could be involved.

of EIU (Goureau et al., 1995) which is responsible to the production
of peroxynitrite anion (ONOO–). Peroxynitrite can cause lipid

In conclusion, PEA is a protective endogenous mediator pro-
duced during inﬂammatory conditions to counteract inﬂamma-
tion, neuronal damage and pain. This study demonstrates that PEA
treatment is able to attenuate the eye inﬂammation induced by
LPS in the rat. Importantly, PEA was effective in counteracting the
uveitis-inducing effects of LPS, when administered, 1 h before and
7 h after challenge with LPS. The later time is a point when
inﬂammatory responses, including TNF-α and NO release in the
aqueous humor, have already commenced (Medeiros et al., 2008).
In addition, in our studies, in spite of the well-known efﬁcacy of
steroidal therapy in uveitis patients, the same results were
obtained for the positive controls DEX-treated rats.
Thus, these protective effects of PEA may be due to the
inhibition of some speciﬁc cytokines which are under the control
of NF-κB pathway. Anyway, the results of the present study
suggest that PEA could have an important role as therapeutic
target in ocular inﬂammation and could be an interesting agent for
the treatment of human uveitis.
