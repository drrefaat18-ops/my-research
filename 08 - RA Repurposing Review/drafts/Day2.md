## 4.1.2. Pioglitazone
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1b-PPAR-agonists]] · 🎯 ≥450 كلمة (حد أدنى)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:
> 

Pioglitazone, a thiazolidinedione, activates the nuclear receptor peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR)-γ, and although it is mainly used as an antidiabetic drug, it may also have anti-inflammatory effects. PPAR-γ activation suppresses pro-inflammatory genes and the production of inflammatory cytokines, matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), and reactive oxygen species (ROS) [Shahin2011]. Another reason to test it in RA is that insulin resistance and inflammation are mutually reinforcing [Ormseth2013].

In animals, pioglitazone was effective only at high doses or when combined with methotrexate. In the adjuvant-induced arthritis (AIA) rat model, oral pioglitazone reduced disease severity, but only at the top dose of 30 mg/kg/day [Koufany2008]. It reduced bone erosions and inflammation-associated bone loss but failed to prevent cartilage lesions, and these anti-arthritic doses were higher than those needed to restore insulin sensitivity and increased body fat [Koufany2008]. In a later study using the same model, pioglitazone alone (1.35 mg/kg/day) had no significant effect on disease activity [Roy2017]. Combined with methotrexate (MTX; 0.225 mg/kg/day), it lowered disease activity more than either drug alone (P < 0.05) [Roy2017].

Clinical trials show a small benefit at best. An Egyptian nonrandomized, single-blind trial enrolled 49 patients with RA and type 2 diabetes, all maintained on MTX 15 mg/week [Shahin2011]. The 28 patients with more active disease added oral pioglitazone 30 mg/day for 12 weeks, while the other 21 took placebo [Shahin2011]. At 12 weeks, DAS28 (P = 0.001) and CRP (P < 0.0001) were lower in the pioglitazone group than in the placebo group, but ESR stayed higher, and weight gain occurred in 25% of treated patients [Shahin2011]. The placebo group also improved from baseline, and the two groups did not start with the same disease activity, which weakens this result [Shahin2011].

In a US crossover trial that was randomized, double-blind and placebo-controlled, 34 patients with RA on stable therapy received pioglitazone 45 mg/day and placebo for 8 weeks each [Ormseth2013]. Pioglitazone produced a modest 9.3% fall in DAS28-CRP (P = 0.046) and no significant change in DAS28 using erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR; P = 0.92), and lower-extremity edema occurred in 16% of participants while on pioglitazone but in none while on placebo [Ormseth2013].

A larger crossover trial with the same design and dose enrolled 143 non-diabetic patients with RA for 3 months per treatment arm, and DAS28-CRP decreased by a mean of 0.31 on pioglitazone compared with 0.15 on placebo (P = 0.02) [Marder2013]. However, the authors concluded that these findings did not, at that stage, support adding PPAR-γ agonists to the standard treatment of RA [Marder2013]. The benefit in these trials was small and came mainly from CRP-based scores, not from ESR or joint counts [Ormseth2013][Marder2013]. Given the risk of fluid retention and weight gain, pioglitazone should not be used routinely in RA, although it may still be studied as an add-on for patients with RA who also have insulin resistance or type 2 diabetes.



## 4.3. Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.3-ARBs-Losartan]] · 🎯 ≥300 كلمة (حد أدنى)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:
> 

The renin–angiotensin system (RAS) regulates blood pressure and fluid balance, and its overactivity is a common cause of hypertension. Several studies suggest that the RAS also contributes to RA, particularly through angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), which is overexpressed in the peripheral blood and synovium of patients with RA [Wang2013].

Angiotensin II acts mainly on angiotensin II type 1 receptors (AT1R). Through these receptors, it promotes ROS production and activates NF-κB, which then increases the release of several pro-inflammatory cytokines [Mostafa2020]. Angiotensin II type 2 receptors (AT2R) oppose these actions, and their stimulation has been linked to anti-inflammatory and tissue-protective effects [Wang2013].

Animal and cell studies of losartan are largely consistent. In AIA rats, losartan at 10 and 15 mg/kg significantly lowered the polyarthritis index compared with vehicle, although this effect was smaller than that of MTX [Wang2013]. Losartan also shifted the receptor balance in the spleen and synovium towards AT2R, with less AT1R and more AT2R, a change that paralleled the fall in the polyarthritis index [Wang2013]. In a second study in AIA rats, losartan lowered serum IL-6 and TNF-α compared with the untreated arthritic group; these changes were accompanied by a rise in serum glutathione (GSH) and a fall in serum malondialdehyde (MDA), two markers of oxidative stress [AbdelElGaphar2018]. An in vitro study tested losartan, valsartan, and enalapril on peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) collected from 16 patients with RA [Cardoso2018]. Losartan lowered four cytokines, IL-6, IL-17F, IL-22, and interferon-γ (IFN-γ), whereas valsartan and enalapril had few or no effects, which suggests that this action may not be shared by valsartan [Cardoso2018]. However, only one high concentration (100 μM) was tested, and cells from patients with active disease responded less [Cardoso2018].

The main clinical evidence for this class in RA comes from a trial of another angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB), candesartan. In this Egyptian single-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial, 45 patients with RA (15 per group) were treated for 3 months with add-on candesartan (8 mg/day), atorvastatin (20 mg/day), or placebo [Mostafa2020]. Compared with the control group, candesartan significantly lowered serum IL-1β, TNF-α, and CRP and improved DAS28 and the disability index of the Health Assessment Questionnaire (HAQ-DI), whereas ESR was reduced only relative to baseline [Mostafa2020]. Atorvastatin lowered both MDA and rheumatoid factor (RF), but candesartan changed neither significantly [Mostafa2020]. These findings should be interpreted with caution, because patients were grouped according to their other medical conditions, only 45 of 59 eligible patients were analysed, the groups differed at baseline in several variables, including CRP and HAQ-DI, and follow-up was short [Mostafa2020].

ARBs, particularly losartan, may therefore be a reasonable choice for patients with RA who also need treatment for hypertension, although larger and longer trials of losartan are required to establish any disease-modifying effect.
