## 4.1.2. Pioglitazone
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1b-PPAR-agonists]] · 🎯 ≥450 كلمة (حد أدنى)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:
> 

Pioglitazone, a thiazolidinedione, activates the nuclear receptor peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR)-γ, and although it is mainly used as an antidiabetic drug, it may also have anti-inflammatory effects. In collagen-induced and serum-transfer arthritis models, PPAR-γ activation by pioglitazone promoted mitophagic flux, which inhibited aberrant activation of the NLR family pyrin domain-containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome and reduced the release of IL-1β and IL-18 [Fu2026]. Loss of autophagy, whether genetic or pharmacological, substantially weakened the anti-inflammatory effects of pioglitazone in vitro and in vivo [Fu2026]. Inflammation may also contribute to obesity-induced insulin resistance, although this issue remains debated [Romeo2012].

In animals, pioglitazone was effective only at high doses or when combined with methotrexate. In the adjuvant-induced arthritis (AIA) rat model, oral pioglitazone reduced disease severity, but only at the top dose of 30 mg/kg/day [Koufany2008]. It reduced bone erosions and inflammation-associated bone loss but failed to prevent cartilage lesions, and these anti-arthritic doses were higher than those needed to restore insulin sensitivity and increased body fat [Koufany2008]. Converted with an animal-to-human dose formula, 30 mg/kg/day in rats corresponds to approximately 290 mg/day for a 60-kg adult [Koufany2008][NairJacob2016]. This is more than six times the highest dose given in RA trials, which ranged from 30 to 45 mg/day [Shahin2011][Ormseth2013][Marder2013]. In a later study using the same model, pioglitazone alone (1.35 mg/kg/day) did not significantly change disease activity [Roy2017]. That dose is equivalent to approximately 13 mg/day for a 60-kg adult [Roy2017][NairJacob2016]. This is below the 30–45 mg/day used in RA trials and may explain the absence of a significant effect of pioglitazone alone. Combined with MTX (0.225 mg/kg/day), it lowered disease activity more than either drug alone (P < 0.05) [Roy2017].

Clinical trials show a small benefit at best. In Egypt, a single-blind, nonrandomized trial included 49 patients who had both RA and type 2 diabetes, all maintained on MTX 15 mg/week [Shahin2011]. The 28 patients with more active disease added oral pioglitazone 30 mg/day for 12 weeks, while the other 21 took placebo [Shahin2011]. At 12 weeks, DAS28 (P = 0.001) and CRP (P < 0.0001) were lower in the pioglitazone group than in the placebo group, and weight gain occurred in 25% of treated patients [Shahin2011]. The placebo group also improved from baseline, and the two groups did not start with the same disease activity, which weakens this result [Shahin2011].

In a US crossover trial that was randomized, double-blind and placebo-controlled, 34 patients with RA on stable therapy received pioglitazone 45 mg/day and placebo for 8 weeks each [Ormseth2013]. Pioglitazone produced a modest 9.3% fall in DAS28-CRP (P = 0.046) and no significant change in DAS28 using erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR; P = 0.92), and lower-extremity edema occurred in 16% of participants while on pioglitazone but in none while on placebo [Ormseth2013]. In the same trial, pioglitazone produced a 3.0-mmHg fall in diastolic blood pressure (95% CI −5.7 to −0.2; P = 0.03) [Ormseth2014]. The augmentation index also fell, by 4.7 percentage units (95% CI −7.9 to −1.5; P = 0.004), whereas the reactive hyperemia index (P = 0.46) and pulse wave velocity in the aorta (P = 0.33) did not change significantly [Ormseth2014]. These improvements were independent of pioglitazone's effects on insulin resistance or inflammation, although the P values were not adjusted for multiple comparisons [Ormseth2014].

A larger crossover trial with the same design and dose enrolled 143 non-diabetic patients with RA for 3 months per treatment arm, and DAS28-CRP decreased by a mean of 0.31 on pioglitazone compared with 0.15 on placebo (P = 0.02) [Marder2013]. However, the authors concluded that these findings did not, at that stage, support adding PPAR-γ agonists to the standard treatment of RA [Marder2013]. The benefit in these trials was small and came mainly from CRP-based scores, not from ESR or joint counts [Ormseth2013][Marder2013]. In a nationwide Taiwanese case–control study of patients with type 2 diabetes, including 3,605 patients with RA and 14,420 controls, thiazolidinedione use was associated with lower odds of RA that did not reach significance (adjusted odds ratio 0.91, 95% CI 0.81 to 1.02) [Hsieh2021]. A higher cumulative dose was also associated with lower RA risk (P for trend = 0.042) [Hsieh2021]. Given the risk of fluid retention and weight gain, pioglitazone should not be used routinely in RA, although it may still be studied as an add-on for patients with RA who also have insulin resistance or type 2 diabetes.



## 4.3. Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.3-ARBs-Losartan]] · 🎯 ≥300 كلمة (حد أدنى)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:
> 

The renin–angiotensin system (RAS) regulates blood pressure and fluid balance, and its overactivity is a common cause of hypertension. The RAS may also contribute to RA, particularly through angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE). ACE activity released by peripheral blood monocytes was raised in 29 of 32 patients with RA [Goto1990]. In synovium from 7 patients with RA, stromal ACE immunostaining and ACE-inhibitor binding were higher than in osteoarthritis or chondromalacia patellae, whereas vascular binding did not differ [Walsh2000].

In rats with arthritis induced by complete Freund's adjuvant (CFA), protein expression of angiotensin II type 1 receptors (AT1R) was higher and that of angiotensin II type 2 receptors (AT2R) was lower than in normal rats (P < 0.05) [Hussein2025]. Conversely, when the AT2R agonist CGP42112 (10 and 20 μg/kg) was injected into the joint of AIA rats, the arthritis index of the treated paw fell on days 22 and 26 [Wang2013].

Animal and cell studies of losartan are largely consistent. In AIA rats, losartan at 10 and 15 mg/kg/day significantly lowered the polyarthritis index compared with vehicle, although this effect was smaller than that of MTX [Wang2013]. When converted to a 60-kg adult with the Km ratio method, these oral doses correspond to an estimated 97 and 146 mg/day [Wang2013][NairJacob2016]. Losartan also shifted the receptor balance in the spleen and synovium towards AT2R, with less AT1R and more AT2R, a change that paralleled the fall in the polyarthritis index [Wang2013]. In a second study in AIA rats, losartan lowered serum IL-6 and TNF-α compared with the vehicle-treated arthritic group; these changes were accompanied by a rise in glutathione (GSH) and a fall in serum malondialdehyde (MDA), two markers of oxidative stress [AbdelElGaphar2018]. A third study, by a different group, used CFA-induced arthritis in rats and tested oral losartan at 20 mg/kg/day [Hussein2025]. Relative to untreated CFA rats, oral losartan reduced angiotensin II (Ang II) in the joint from 170.59 to 22.41 pg/g, IL-6 from 320.78 to 129.43 pg/g, and TNF-α from 325.80 to 130.99 pg/g [Hussein2025]. In the same study, oral losartan also lowered joint tissue nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidase from 75.10 to 18.29 ng/g and nitric oxide (NO) from 37.32 to 10.53 μmol/g [Hussein2025]. In one in vitro study of stimulated mononuclear cells from 16 patients with RA, losartan (100 μM) lowered interferon-γ (IFN-γ), IL-6, IL-17F, and IL-22 [Cardoso2018]. However, cytokines were measured at a single concentration (100 μM), and the response was weaker in more active disease, with only IL-22 still reduced at DAS28 > 3.2 [Cardoso2018]. These results may therefore serve only as supporting evidence.

The main clinical evidence for this class in RA comes from a trial of another angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB), candesartan. In this Egyptian single-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial, 45 patients with RA (15 per group) were treated for 3 months with add-on candesartan (8 mg/day), atorvastatin (20 mg/day), or placebo [Mostafa2020]. Compared with the control group, candesartan significantly lowered serum IL-1β, TNF-α, and CRP and improved DAS28 and the disability index of the Health Assessment Questionnaire (HAQ-DI), whereas ESR was reduced only relative to baseline [Mostafa2020]. Atorvastatin lowered both MDA and rheumatoid factor (RF), but candesartan changed neither significantly [Mostafa2020]. These findings should be interpreted with caution, because patients were grouped according to their other medical conditions, only 45 of 59 eligible patients were analysed, the groups differed at baseline in several variables, including CRP and HAQ-DI, and follow-up was short [Mostafa2020].

ARBs, particularly losartan, may therefore be a reasonable choice for patients with RA who also need treatment for hypertension, although larger and longer trials of losartan are required to establish any disease-modifying effect.
