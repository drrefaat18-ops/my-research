## 4.1.2. PPAR Agonists: Pioglitazone and Fenofibrate
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1b-PPAR-agonists]] · 🎯 ~450 كلمة
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:
> 

Pioglitazone, a thiazolidinedione, activates the nuclear receptor PPAR-γ (peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-gamma). Although it is mainly used as an antidiabetic drug, it may also have anti-inflammatory effects. PPAR-γ activation reduces the expression of pro-inflammatory genes, and this lowers the production of inflammatory cytokines, matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), and reactive oxygen species (ROS) [Shahin2011]. Another reason to test it in RA is that insulin resistance and inflammation make each other worse [Ormseth2013].

In the adjuvant-induced arthritis (AIA) rat model, oral pioglitazone reduced disease severity, but only at the top dose of 30 mg/kg/day [Koufany2008]. It reduced bone erosions and inflammation-associated bone loss but failed to prevent cartilage lesions [Koufany2008]. These anti-arthritic doses were also higher than those needed to restore insulin sensitivity, and they increased body fat [Koufany2008]. In a later study using the same model, pioglitazone monotherapy (1.35 mg/kg/day) had no significant effect on disease activity, whereas its combination with methotrexate (MTX; 0.225 mg/kg/day) suppressed disease activity more than either agent alone (P < 0.05) [Roy2017].

In patients, an Egyptian non-randomized, single-blind trial enrolled 49 patients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA) and type 2 diabetes mellitus, all maintained on MTX 15 mg/week [Shahin2011]. The 28 patients with more active disease added oral pioglitazone 30 mg/day for 12 weeks, while the other 21 took placebo [Shahin2011]. Compared with placebo, pioglitazone produced significantly larger reductions in the DAS28 disease activity score (P = 0.001) and in C-reactive protein (CRP; P < 0.0001), although weight gain was reported in 25% of treated patients [Shahin2011].

In a US crossover trial that was randomized, double-blind and placebo-controlled, 34 patients with RA on stable therapy received pioglitazone 45 mg/day and placebo for 8 weeks each [Ormseth2013]. Pioglitazone produced a modest 9.3% reduction in CRP-based DAS28 (P = 0.046) but no improvement in DAS28 based on the erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) (P = 0.92) [Ormseth2013]. Lower-extremity edema occurred in 16% of participants while on pioglitazone but in none while on placebo [Ormseth2013].

A larger crossover trial with the same design and dose enrolled 143 non-diabetic patients with RA, with 3 months per treatment arm [Marder2013]. DAS28-CRP decreased by a mean of 0.31 on pioglitazone compared with 0.15 on placebo (P = 0.02) [Marder2013]. However, the authors concluded that these findings did not, at that stage, support adding PPAR-γ agonists to the standard treatment of RA [Marder2013]. The benefit in these trials was small and came mainly from CRP-based scores, not from a clear fall in joint counts. Given the risk of fluid retention and weight gain, pioglitazone should not be used routinely in RA; however, it may still be studied as an add-on for RA patients who also have insulin resistance or type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Fenofibrate is a widely used PPAR-α agonist prescribed primarily for the treatment of hypertriglyceridemia [Zhou2012]. In vitro, fenofibrate inhibited Th17-cell differentiation in murine T cells in a concentration-dependent manner over the range of 5–20 μM, producing a 74% reduction at 20 μM [Zhou2012]. However, other PPAR-α agonists, including WY14643, GW7647, and bezafibrate, had no effect on Th17-cell differentiation, suggesting that the inhibitory effect of fenofibrate may be independent of PPAR-α activation [Zhou2012]. In rats with adjuvant-induced arthritis, 12 days of fenofibrate treatment (300 mg/kg/day) reduced the arthritis score from 11.0 to 5.83, corresponding to an approximately 47% reduction in the external signs of inflammation (P < 0.01) [Castillero2012]. In patients with RA, clinical data are limited to one randomized trial in which fenofibrate performed similarly to a statin on ESR, CRP, and DAS28 [Xing2015]. Placebo-controlled trials are therefore needed to test whether these anti-inflammatory effects seen in animals also help RA patients.


## 4.3. Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.3-ARBs-Losartan]] · 🎯 ~300 كلمة
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:
> 

The renin–angiotensin system (RAS) regulates blood pressure and fluid balance, and its overactivity is a well-known cause of hypertension. However, some studies suggest that RAS also contributes to RA, particularly through angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), which is overexpressed in the peripheral blood and synovium of RA patients [Wang2013].

Angiotensin II stimulates angiotensin II type 1 receptors (AT1R), triggering the generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and activation of nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB), which then promotes the release of multiple pro-inflammatory cytokines [Mostafa2020]. In contrast, angiotensin II type 2 receptors (AT2R) work against AT1R, and their stimulation has been linked to anti-inflammatory and tissue-protective effects [Wang2013].

In a preclinical study, adjuvant-induced arthritis (AIA) rats treated with losartan at doses of 10 and 15 mg/kg had a significant reduction in their polyarthritis index compared with vehicle-treated rats, although this improvement was smaller than that seen with methotrexate (MTX) [Wang2013]. Losartan also shifted the balance between the two receptors, with less AT1R and more AT2R in the spleen and synovium, and this shift tracked the fall in the polyarthritis index [Wang2013]. Another study on AIA rats found that losartan lowered serum IL-6 and TNF-α compared with the untreated arthritic group, together with a rise in serum glutathione (GSH) and a fall in serum malondialdehyde (MDA) [AbdelElGaphar2018]. An in vitro study tested losartan, valsartan, and enalapril on peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) collected from 16 RA patients. Only losartan clearly lowered four cytokines (IL-6, IL-17F, IL-22, and IFN-γ), while valsartan and enalapril did little, which points to an effect of losartan itself rather than of the whole class [Cardoso2018].

The main clinical evidence for this class in RA comes from a trial of another ARB, candesartan. In this Egyptian single-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial, 45 patients with RA (15 per group) were treated for 3 months with add-on candesartan (8 mg/day), atorvastatin (20 mg/day), or placebo [Mostafa2020]. Candesartan significantly lowered serum IL-1β, TNF-α, CRP, and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), and it improved DAS28 and HAQ-DI disability scores compared with both baseline and the control group [Mostafa2020]. However, unlike the antioxidant effect of losartan in rats, candesartan failed to produce significant changes in MDA and rheumatoid factor (RF), whereas atorvastatin significantly lowered both markers [Mostafa2020]. The interpretation of these findings is further limited by baseline imbalances between the groups in several clinical variables and by the small sample size [Mostafa2020].

ARBs, particularly losartan, may represent a reasonable antihypertensive option for patients with RA and hypertension; however, larger and longer-term randomized controlled trials, especially those evaluating losartan, are needed to confirm these findings.
