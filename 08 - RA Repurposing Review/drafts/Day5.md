## 1. Introduction
> 📖 الإملاء: [[1-Introduction]] · 🎯 ≥500 كلمة (حد أدنى)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA), a chronic inflammatory disorder of autoimmune origin, is linked to disability and early death [GBD2023]. RA affected an estimated 17.6 million people of all ages worldwide in 2020, with a 95% uncertainty interval (UI) of 15.8–20.3, which is 121% more than in 1990 [GBD2023]. Adjustment for age did not remove this rise: global prevalence reached 208.8 per 100,000 population, 14.1% above the 1990 level, and females were affected more often than males, with a prevalence ratio of 2.45 after age standardisation [GBD2023]. RA accounted for 3.06 million global disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) in 2020 (95% UI 2.32–3.86), and years lived with disability (YLDs) made up 76.4% of this total (95% UI 68.3–81.0) [GBD2023]. However, the global age-standardised DALY rate showed no significant change between 1990 and 2020 (−1.0%; 95% UI −5.5 to 2.2), yet it rose by 27.8% in North Africa and the Middle East [GBD2023]. By 2050, forecasts estimate 31.7 million people living with RA worldwide, with a 95% UI of 25.8–39.0, which is 80.2% more than in 2020 [GBD2023].

A Saudi prospective cohort followed 647 patients on disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) of biological or targeted synthetic type (b/tsDMARDs) under treat-to-target management, and only those who completed all three visits were analysed [Almoallim2026]. Remission rates, defined by a score under 2.6 on a 28-joint disease activity measure that uses C-reactive protein (DAS28-CRP), were 50.9%, 68.0% and 83.5% at the first, second and third visits [Almoallim2026]. About one in six of these patients was still not in remission at the third visit [Almoallim2026]. Between 10.5% and 18.7% of patients in cohort and registry studies were classified as having difficult-to-treat (D2T) RA [Natalucci2025; VanMullem2026; AlRayes2026]. This range probably reflects differences in population, design and follow-up. The lowest figure came from a single-centre Brussels cohort of early RA (41 of 391 patients after 5 years) [Natalucci2025], and the highest came from a second Brussels cohort of patients exposed to at least one b/tsDMARD (126 of 675; mean follow-up 217 months) [VanMullem2026]. In a cross-sectional Saudi registry, 126 of 928 patients (13.6%) met the criteria for D2T RA [AlRayes2026]. Because the two Brussels cohorts share a centre, their patients may overlap. D2T RA must satisfy three requirements set by the European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR), the first being treatment failure on two or more b/tsDMARDs that differ in mechanism after earlier failure of conventional synthetic DMARDs (csDMARDs), unless these are contraindicated [Nagy2021]. The second is at least one indicator of ongoing disease, such as at least moderate disease activity or signs suggestive of active disease [Nagy2021]. The third is management judged problematic by the rheumatologist, the patient or both [Nagy2021]. In the Brussels early-RA cohort, baseline radiographic damage was the sole factor retained as independent in the multivariable model of progression to D2T RA, with an odds ratio (OR) of 2.35 and a 95% confidence interval (CI) of 1.13–5.41 [Natalucci2025]. In the second Brussels cohort, glucocorticoid exposure before b/tsDMARD treatment was associated with progression to D2T RA in the post-2000 subcohort of 498 patients, with a hazard ratio (HR) of 2.47 (95% CI 1.38–4.42) [VanMullem2026]. However, glucocorticoid use may mark more severe underlying disease rather than act as a cause. In the cross-sectional Saudi registry, D2T RA was associated with greater disease activity, more functional disability, a raised erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) and poorer adherence to treatment [AlRayes2026]. Because this registry study was cross-sectional, it cannot establish which of these came first [AlRayes2026]. A cost-effectiveness model set in Zanzibar and limited to outpatient costs from a health-provider perspective estimated that methotrexate alone cost US$634 per patient over 3 years [Said2025]. Methotrexate followed by two consecutive b/tsDMARDs cost US$5,011 [Said2025]. These prices may not apply to Egypt. A systematic review of 15 studies from 12 low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) reported a pooled average annual medical cost of US$3,379.83 (95% CI 3,137.58–3,622.08) [Gebrye2026]. Only nine of these studies supplied medical-cost data, and their estimates lay between US$1,728 and US$7,584 per patient annually, which suggests wide variation between settings [Gebrye2026].

Developing a new drug is costly. A US economic model for 2000–2018 estimated a mean expected capitalized cost of US$879.3 million per new drug once failures and the cost of capital were included [Sertkaya2024]. A second model examined research and development (R&D) spending on 38 drugs licensed in 2019 by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) of the United States [Mulcahy2025]. Their median cost was US$708 million, whereas the mean, at US$1.31 billion, was pulled upward by a few very costly drugs [Mulcahy2025]. Drug repurposing applies licensed compounds, with largely known safety and pharmacokinetics, to new indications. A dataset of FDA Center for Drug Evaluation and Research approvals from 1985 to 2024 identified 451 drugs later approved for a new use, on average 7.2 years after first approval [Akodad2026]. In addition, cardiovascular and metabolic comorbidity in RA may give a further reason to examine drugs already licensed for these conditions, since a meta-analysis of observational studies reported that RA was associated with a higher risk of myocardial infarction (MI) [Barkhane2023]. For MI, the risk ratio (RR) was 1.43 with a 95% CI of 1.29 to 1.57 across 13 studies, while stroke risk was 1.30 times higher across 7 studies, although heterogeneity was high in both analyses (I² 84% and 91%) [Barkhane2023]. Study-level meta-regression in the same analysis found that diabetes, measured across studies rather than in individual patients, significantly explained part of the variation in cardiovascular disease and MI risk [Barkhane2023]. Section 4.1 discusses the evidence on metformin and the glitazones (thiazolidinediones) in more detail. In a two-sample Mendelian randomization study, variation in the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARG) gene, which encodes the thiazolidinedione target, was inversely associated with RA risk after correction for multiple testing [Qin2023]. The odds ratio was 0.38 for each 0.1 mmol/L of glucose lowering (P = 0.004; 95% CI 0.20 to 0.73) [Qin2023].

The reviews we identified have mostly examined one drug class at a time. A systematic review and meta-analysis limited to metformin added to csDMARDs pooled four randomized controlled trials of patients with active RA, all conducted in Egypt or India [Alshammari2026]. These trials lasted 12 weeks to 6 months [Alshammari2026]. The review authors rated two of the four at high risk of bias, mainly because both were single-blinded and did not report a registration number [Alshammari2026]. For statins, a systematic review and meta-analysis that included 19 studies found lower values of the 28-joint disease activity score, ESR and C-reactive protein in patients with RA [Ren2023]. Tender joint counts, total cholesterol and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol also fell, whereas changes in interleukin-6 and swollen joints did not reach significance, and heterogeneity in the disease activity estimate was high (I² = 86.0%) [Ren2023]. An Egger test suggested publication bias for this outcome [Ren2023]. A separate meta-analysis asked whether statin use alters the chance of developing RA, rather than how well it treats the disease [Pan2024]. No association with statin use was detected across nine pooled studies, eight of which were observational, while the single randomized trial was stopped early because of low recruitment and included only 62 patients [Pan2024]. Of two scoping reviews on glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists, the first centred on RA and judged that the evidence in this disease remained mostly observational and of variable design, too limited to settle efficacy or causality [Buonanno2026]. Its authors noted that improvements in inflammatory outcomes might partly reflect metabolic effects such as weight loss and better insulin sensitivity, not only direct immunomodulation [Buonanno2026]. The second covered several rheumatic diseases and drew on 52 studies and seven clinical trials, most of which carried a risk of bias, and found that clinical impact in RA and psoriatic arthritis remained partly linked to weight loss [Bilgin2025]. To our knowledge, no earlier review has considered antidiabetic, lipid-lowering and antihypertensive drugs together in RA, converted animal doses into human equivalent doses, assessed the safety of these classes alongside methotrexate and glucocorticoids, or compared the drugs by shared pathway.


## 5. Challenges and Future Perspectives

> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:

These drugs would likely be added to csDMARDs such as MTX, sometimes with glucocorticoids; their safety may therefore depend on this background therapy. In TRACE RA, where 86% of patients took csDMARDs and 17% received glucocorticoids, muscle pain reports (132 with atorvastatin vs 117 with placebo) and liver enzyme elevations of 2–5 times the upper limit of normal (90 vs 69 cases) did not differ significantly [Kitas2019]. Creatine kinase and alanine aminotransferase levels were significantly higher with atorvastatin, by about 12–15%, which the authors judged clinically insignificant [Kitas2019]. When metformin was added to low-dose MTX, adverse-event rates did not differ from placebo and no serious adverse effects were recorded [Abdallah2021]. Among people with RA and type 2 diabetes starting antidiabetic drugs, urinary tract infection (UTI) rates did not differ significantly between SGLT2 inhibitors and other antidiabetic drugs by intention to treat [Dobashi2026]. An excess appeared in the as-treated analysis (adjusted incidence rate ratio of 1.64, 95% CI 1.26 to 2.13), with no significant glucocorticoid-dose interaction [Dobashi2026]. In a Japanese study of immune-mediated diseases, mostly RA, with type 2 diabetes, no significant UTI difference was found between SGLT2 inhibitors and metformin, and no glucocorticoid interaction was detected, although the intervals were wide [Tsushima2026]. Genital infection, a secondary outcome, was more common with SGLT2 inhibitors (HR of 2.31, 95% CI 1.16 to 4.63) [Tsushima2026]. In rats given MTX, empagliflozin, an SGLT2 inhibitor, lessened liver injury, which is preclinical supporting evidence only [Kalantari2024]. It also lowered serum aminotransferases [Kalantari2024].

In the GLP-1RA chart review, 29% stopped treatment, mostly for gastrointestinal adverse effects (15% of patients) or insurance issues [Kellner2025]. In a US crossover trial, body weight rose by 0.76 kg on pioglitazone compared with placebo (95% CI 0.10–1.42), and edema was also reported [Ormseth2013]. Some patients stopped fenofibrate because of severe back pain possibly related to it (Section 4.2.2) [Shirinsky2013]. With candesartan, liver enzymes and kidney function did not change significantly, whereas aminotransferases rose with atorvastatin but stayed within the normal range [Mostafa2020]. Semaglutide and liraglutide were associated with new-onset RA, mainly after short treatment (Section 4.1.3) [Israel2026]. However, this concerns disease onset rather than established RA, and the analysis was not adjusted for glucocorticoid use [Israel2026]. Apart from the two glucocorticoid interaction analyses, none of the studies we read tested interactions with MTX. Monitoring may be sensible.
