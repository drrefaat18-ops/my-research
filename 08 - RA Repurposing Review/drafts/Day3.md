## 4.1.3. GLP-1 Receptor Agonists
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1c-GLP1-RA]] · 🎯 ≥300 كلمة (حد أدنى)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:

Liraglutide, semaglutide and dulaglutide activate the receptor for glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) and are therefore called GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs); GLP-1 raises insulin and lowers glucagon secretion in a glucose-dependent manner, slows gastric emptying and reduces food intake [Drucker2018]. Several lines of evidence link these drugs to RA. In a cross-sectional study of 151 patients with RA but without diabetes and 210 sex-matched controls, the patients had an impaired incretin-insulin axis, with higher glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and insulin levels, higher GLP-1 levels after multivariable adjustment and more insulin resistance [TejeraSegura2017]. A review reports that GLP-1 receptors are expressed on circulating T and B lymphocytes in mice and humans and are strongly detectable on human monocytes, but that expression wanes soon after differentiation into macrophages [Bendotti2022]. These findings are not settled, because another review states that most immune cell subtypes lack functional GLP-1 receptors [Drucker2018]. Immunohistochemical methods for detecting the receptor often lack specificity, and results differ between humans and rodents [Bendotti2022]. In cultured human fibroblast-like synoviocytes from the knee synovium of 15 donors, stimulated with TNF-α (10 ng/mL), dulaglutide (50 or 100 nM, 24 h) lowered expression of IL-1β, IL-6, the chemokine monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 and the alarmin high-mobility group box 1 [Zheng2019]. At the protein level, these cytokines returned close to baseline, although IL-6 mRNA remained about 2-fold above basal levels [Zheng2019]. Dulaglutide also reduced the rise in the degradative enzymes matrix metalloproteinase-3 and -13 [Zheng2019]. In related assays, these effects were accompanied by lower activation of c-Jun N-terminal kinase and by a return of nuclear p65, a nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) subunit, to near-baseline levels [Zheng2019]. A review also cites a hypothesis, put forward for osteoarthritis rather than RA, that GLP-1RAs may favor an anti-inflammatory M2 macrophage phenotype [Bendotti2022].

Animal data now add to these cell findings. In AIA rats induced with complete Freund's adjuvant, liraglutide was given at 75 μg/kg/day intraperitoneally from day 15 to day 56 after induction [Nematalla2026]. It inhibited signaling through NF-κB, the NLRP3 inflammasome and IL-1β, as well as through TNF-α and p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase [Nematalla2026]. The authors report significant therapeutic efficacy in this model, with more pronounced effects when liraglutide was combined with MTX [Nematalla2026]. By body surface area conversion with Km factors of 6 for rats and 37 for humans, this dose corresponds to a human equivalent dose of approximately 12 μg/kg, or about 0.7 mg/day for a 60 kg adult [NairJacob2016]. This lies between the 0.6 mg/day starting dose, which is given only for the first week of titration, and the 1.8 mg/day maximum for type 2 diabetes, and below the 3 mg/day used for weight management [Victoza2025][Saxenda2026]. The route differs, however, as liraglutide was injected intraperitoneally in rats but is given subcutaneously in humans [Nematalla2026][Victoza2025].

Clinical data on GLP-1RAs in patients with RA are scarce, and the main study is a single-center chart review of overweight or obese patients (BMI ≥ 27 kg/m²) [Kellner2025]. It compared 173 patients who took semaglutide (most patients) or tirzepatide with 42 patients who were prescribed one of these drugs but did not take it [Kellner2025]. Disease activity, graded from clinic notes on a 0–3 scale rather than DAS28, changed by −0.03 versus +0.20 in controls (P = 0.03), a small difference of unclear clinical meaning [Kellner2025]. Pain fell by 0.6 cm on a visual analogue scale in the treated group but rose by 1.3 cm in controls (P < 0.001) [Kellner2025]. ESR and CRP did not fall more than in controls, and the pain relief was not linked to weight loss, but the study was small and the reasons for starting the drug were not measured [Kellner2025].

Cohort data also link GLP-1RAs to fewer cardiorespiratory and thrombotic events in RA. In adults with RA and obesity but no diabetes, semaglutide or tirzepatide use was linked to fewer first episodes of heart failure (HF) or respiratory failure during days 91–365 than no use (0.7% vs 1.8%; HR of 0.48, 95% CI 0.30 to 0.78) [Loizidis2026]. The authors describe these observations as hypothesis-generating [Loizidis2026]. In a retrospective cohort of 8,697 matched pairs of patients with RA and type 2 diabetes, the 5-year risk of any thromboembolic event was 24% lower with GLP-1 analogs than with DPP-4 inhibitors (HR of 0.76, 95% CI 0.70 to 0.83) [WangQ2025]. Pulmonary embolism alone did not differ significantly (HR of 0.90, 95% CI 0.74 to 1.09), and the authors noted as a limitation that body mass index remained higher in the GLP-1 analog group even after matching [WangQ2025]. In an emulated trial of 1,017 matched pairs of obese adults with type 2 diabetes and RA, semaglutide use was associated with fewer major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events than non-GLP-1RA therapy (12.7% vs 16.5%; HR of 0.75, 95% CI 0.60 to 0.94) [Malik2026]. Comparators included DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors, sulfonylureas and thiazolidinediones [Malik2026]. Incident HF was also lower with semaglutide (8.9% vs 12.5%; HR of 0.69, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.91), whereas all-cause mortality, myocardial infarction and stroke did not differ significantly [Malik2026]. The authors attribute the overall benefit mainly to this lower HF risk [Malik2026]. The authors state that the observational design limits causal inference [Malik2026].

Not all data indicate a benefit. A case-control study compared 4,535 RA patients with 22,675 controls and found that use of semaglutide or liraglutide in the 10 years before diagnosis was linked to higher odds of new-onset RA (adjusted odds ratios 1.64 and 1.58) [Israel2026]. Dulaglutide showed a similar but non-significant trend (adjusted odds ratio 1.31) [Israel2026]. This link was seen mainly with short use (≤6 months), which the authors read as a possible transient pro-inflammatory effect early in treatment, although they note that causality cannot be inferred [Israel2026]. However, part of this may reflect confounding by indication or reverse causality, because obesity and diabetes, the main reasons for giving these drugs, are independent risk factors for RA [Israel2026]. The authors add that sustained use may offset this effect through weight loss and better insulin sensitivity [Israel2026]. This case-control study addressed new-onset RA, whereas the chart review above included patients with established RA; the two results are therefore not necessarily in conflict [Israel2026][Kellner2025].

To date, the evidence is limited, observational and mixed, and we found no randomized trials in RA. The rat study and the cohort data on heart failure, respiratory failure and thrombotic events were favorable, but all of them are preclinical or observational. GLP-1RAs may help some patients with RA and obesity, but they cannot be recommended yet. A possible cardiovascular benefit may be an additional reason for trials, which should also check whether the first months of treatment briefly worsen inflammation.

## 4.1.4. SGLT2 Inhibitors
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1d-SGLT2i]] · 🎯 ≥250 كلمة (حد أدنى)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:

SGLT2 inhibitors, such as dapagliflozin, are oral drugs used to improve glycemic control in type 2 diabetes [Farxiga2026]. By inhibiting SGLT2 in the proximal tubule of the kidney, these drugs reduce glucose reabsorption, and the glucose that is not reabsorbed is excreted in the urine [Vallon2024][Farxiga2026]. To our knowledge, the only animal study in arthritis reported clear anti-inflammatory effects. In an Egyptian study (Ain Shams University) of AIA rats induced with complete Freund's adjuvant, three oral daily doses of dapagliflozin (1, 5 and 10 mg/kg) were tested for 3 weeks; the highest dose worked best [ElDemerdash2025]. Converted by body surface area, with Km factors of 6 for rats and 37 for humans, the highest dose in these male Sprague-Dawley rats corresponds to approximately 97 mg/day for a 60 kg adult [ElDemerdash2025][NairJacob2016]. This is about ten times the 10 mg once-daily dose in the dapagliflozin label, whereas the lowest rat dose corresponds to approximately 10 mg/day, close to the label dose [Farxiga2026]. Compared with untreated arthritic rats, dapagliflozin alone lowered serum NF-κB p65 by 66% and TNF-α by 71% [ElDemerdash2025]. It raised the p-AMPK/t-AMPK ratio in the joint by 374%, whereas MTX alone did not change it, which suggests involvement of the AMPK pathway described for metformin (Section 4.1.1) [ElDemerdash2025]. It also raised joint gene expression of the autophagy markers autophagy-related gene 7 (ATG-7), Beclin-1 and unc-51-like kinase 1 (ULK-1) [ElDemerdash2025]. On X-ray, neither drug alone reduced bone erosion or joint-space narrowing; only the two drugs together did [ElDemerdash2025].

Human data on SGLT2 inhibitors and RA onset are scarce; one study used TriNetX records to emulate target trials in adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes [Yen2026]. It compared SGLT2 inhibitors with sulfonylureas, dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) inhibitors or pioglitazone (more than 80,000 matched pairs per comparison) [Yen2026]. In the first year, SGLT2 inhibitor use was linked to a slightly lower risk of new RA than sulfonylurea use (0.118% vs 0.131%; HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.84–0.97), whereas the differences against DPP-4 inhibitors (HR 0.91) and pioglitazone (HR 0.92) were not significant [Yen2026]. The gap with sulfonylureas shrank with longer follow-up and lost significance, as it also did in a per-protocol analysis [Yen2026]. The authors suggest that one year may be too short, because RA has a long silent phase before diagnosis [Yen2026].

Unlike GLP-1RAs (Section 4.1.3), SGLT2 inhibitors were not linked to a higher RA risk than other glucose-lowering drugs [Yen2026]. Clinical data in patients who already have RA are scarce and inconclusive. In a cohort of 277 matched pairs of adults with RA and type 2 diabetes, SGLT2 inhibitor use was associated with lower odds of major adverse cardiovascular events than DPP-4 inhibitor use, but not significantly so (odds ratio of 0.792, 95% CI 0.567 to 1.107) [Kachhadia2026]. The absolute risk difference was −5.8 percentage points (P = 0.172), post-hoc power was approximately 40–50%, and follow-up differed markedly between groups (medians of 3.1 and 6.4 years) [Kachhadia2026]. The authors consider the findings hypothesis-generating [Kachhadia2026]. A 2024 review noted that clinical evidence on SGLT2 inhibitors in rheumatic diseases is limited, and the articles it covered dealt with systemic lupus erythematosus, gout and lupus nephritis [Nallathambi2024]. In Egypt, two randomized trials of SGLT2 inhibitors added to MTX in patients with RA have been registered, both with DAS28-CRP as the primary outcome, but neither had started recruiting at the time of writing [Tanta2024][AlAzhar2026]. Their use in RA therefore remains experimental.

## 4.1.5. DPP-4 Inhibitors
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.4-Emerging]] (تعديلات خطوة 11)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:

In diabetes cohorts, DPP-4 inhibitors have served mainly as the comparator group rather than as the treatment under study. They were the control arm for GLP-1RAs in 8,697 matched patients per group [WangQ2025] and in 4,607 propensity score-matched patients per group [Yu2026]. Similarly, they were compared with SGLT2 inhibitors in 284 versus 823 patients before matching [Kachhadia2026] and in 206,069 matched pairs [Yen2026], and they formed part of the comparator group in a semaglutide cohort [Malik2026]. In RA with type 2 diabetes, GLP-1RAs were associated with lower all-cause mortality than DPP-4 inhibitors (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.58 to 0.80) [Yu2026]. Major cardiovascular events, however, did not differ between the two groups (HR 0.96, 95% CI 0.89 to 1.04) [Yu2026]. In the primary analysis of one population-based cohort, initiating a DPP-4 inhibitor was linked to a modestly lower RA risk than metformin plus another non-DPP-4 agent (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.44 to 0.99) [Kim2015]. However, when thiazolidinedione or sulfonylurea initiators served as the reference, the lower risk did not reach significance (HRs 1.04 [0.51–2.12] and 0.66 [0.38–1.15], respectively) [Kim2015]. The authors also noted that residual confounding could not be excluded [Kim2015]. By contrast, a nested case-control study of diabetic patients (790 RA cases, 11,850 controls) found no significant association with RA after adjustment (adjusted OR 1.16, 95% CI 0.94 to 1.43) [Kathe2018]. Only the non-insulin subgroup showed a positive association (OR 1.30, 95% CI 1.001 to 1.985), which is at most a signal because the lower limit barely exceeds 1 [Kathe2018].

The laboratory data we found are limited and conflicting. In an Egyptian study conducted in Alexandria, oral sitagliptin (100 mg/kg/day) reduced hind paw swelling in AIA rats compared with untreated animals [Ibrahim2020]. Moreover, sitagliptin lowered serum TNF-α, IL-6, anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide (anti-CCP) antibodies and receptor activator of NF-κB ligand (RANKL), a driver of osteoclast formation, relative to the untreated arthritic group (P < 0.001) [Ibrahim2020]. Its effect on the clinical arthrogram score, which was assessed by examination rather than imaging, and on synovial phosphorylated STAT3 (p-STAT3) protein was smaller than that of tofacitinib, which was tested in parallel [Ibrahim2020]. A contrasting in vitro report found lower DPP-4 levels in RA than in normal synovial tissue, and sitagliptin and vildagliptin raised the release of proinflammatory cytokines from RA synovial fibroblasts [Han2021]. The authors inferred a possible increase in RA risk from these cell experiments [Han2021]. The evidence therefore rests on conflicting observational studies and on animal and in vitro work, and we found no randomized trial of DPP-4 inhibitors in RA in the registries searched.
