## 4.1.3. GLP-1 Receptor Agonists
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1c-GLP1-RA]] · 🎯 ~300 كلمة
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:

Liraglutide, semaglutide and dulaglutide are receptor agonists of glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1RAs). They raise insulin release when glucose is high, lower glucagon, slow stomach emptying and increase fullness [Israel2026]. Their link to RA comes from two sides. Patients with RA but without diabetes have an impaired incretin-insulin axis, as found in 151 patients compared with 210 sex-matched controls [TejeraSegura2017]. GLP-1 receptors are also found on immune cells (T and B cells, monocytes and macrophages), where the drugs lower TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-1β, block nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) and favor M2 (anti-inflammatory) macrophages [Israel2026].

Clinical data in RA come mainly from a single-center chart review of overweight or obese patients (BMI ≥ 27 kg/m²) that compared 173 patients who took semaglutide or tirzepatide (a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist) with 42 patients who were prescribed one of these drugs but never took it [Kellner2025]. Disease activity, graded from clinic notes on a 0–3 scale rather than DAS28, changed by −0.03 versus +0.20 in controls (P = 0.03), which is probably too small to matter clinically [Kellner2025]. Pain fell by 0.6 cm on a visual analogue scale in the treated group but rose by 1.3 cm in controls (P < 0.001) [Kellner2025]. ESR and CRP did not fall more than in controls, and the pain relief was not linked to weight loss [Kellner2025]. The study was small, and the reasons for starting the drug were not measured [Kellner2025].

Not all data point to a benefit. In a case-control study of 4,535 RA patients and 22,675 controls, use of semaglutide or liraglutide in the 10 years before diagnosis was linked to higher odds of new-onset RA (adjusted odds ratios 1.64 and 1.58), but dulaglutide was not [Israel2026]. This link was seen mainly with short use (≤6 months), and the authors suggest the drugs may cause a short-lived rise in inflammation early in treatment [Israel2026]. Confounding may explain part of this, because obesity and diabetes, the main reasons for giving these drugs, raise RA risk on their own [Israel2026]. The authors add that longer use may lower inflammation through weight loss and better insulin sensitivity [Israel2026]. This could explain why patients in the Kellner study, followed for up to a year, had less pain rather than worse disease.

So far, the evidence is small, observational and mixed, and we found no randomized trials in RA. GLP-1RAs may help some patients with RA and obesity, but they cannot be recommended yet, and future trials should check whether the first months of treatment briefly worsen inflammation.

## 4.1.4. SGLT2 Inhibitors
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1d-SGLT2i]] · 🎯 ~250 كلمة
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:

SGLT2 inhibitors are oral drugs for type 2 diabetes; dapagliflozin, for example, blocks sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) in the kidney, so less glucose goes back into the blood [ElDemerdash2025]. In AIA rats (induced with complete Freund's adjuvant), three oral daily doses of dapagliflozin (1, 5 and 10 mg/kg) were tested for 3 weeks; the highest dose worked best [ElDemerdash2025]. Compared with untreated arthritic rats, dapagliflozin alone lowered serum NF-κB p65 by 66% and TNF-α by 71% [ElDemerdash2025]. It raised the p-AMPK/t-AMPK ratio in the joint by 374%, while MTX alone did not change it [ElDemerdash2025]. This is the same AMPK pathway described for metformin (Section 4.1.1). The drug also switched on autophagy: ATG-7, Beclin-1 and ULK-1 rose in the joint [ElDemerdash2025]. On X-ray, neither drug alone reduced bone erosion or joint-space narrowing; only the two drugs together did [ElDemerdash2025].

In humans, a study used TriNetX records to emulate target trials in adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes, comparing SGLT2 inhibitors with sulfonylureas, dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) inhibitors or pioglitazone (more than 80,000 matched pairs per comparison) [Yen2026]. In the first year, new RA was slightly less common with SGLT2 inhibitors than with sulfonylureas (hazard ratio [HR] 0.90, 95% CI 0.84–0.97) [Yen2026]. Against DPP-4 inhibitors (HR 0.91) or pioglitazone (HR 0.92), the difference was not significant [Yen2026]. The gap with sulfonylureas shrank with longer follow-up and lost significance [Yen2026]. The authors point out that one year is short, because RA has a long silent phase before diagnosis [Yen2026]. These data also concern RA prevention in people with diabetes, not treatment of established RA.

Unlike GLP-1RAs (Section 4.1.3), SGLT2 inhibitors were not linked to a higher RA risk. Still, we found only one animal study and one observational study, and no trial in patients who already have RA. SGLT2 inhibitors need more animal work and randomized trials in RA before any use in patients.

## 4.4. Emerging Candidates
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.4-Emerging]] · 🎯 ~200 كلمة
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:

Several other drugs have been proposed for RA, but their evidence is weak, indirect or mixed (Table 2). In rats with collagen-induced arthritis (CIA), the non-selective β-blocker propranolol reduced inflammation in the heart and lowered its vulnerability to arrhythmias [Lin2020]. Its effect on the joints was small: paw swelling and clinical scores stayed close to those of untreated CIA rats and improved only mildly late in the disease [Lin2020]. In pooled trial data from 5,502 RA patients, the 594 (10.8%) who used β-blockers, mostly β1-selective ones, reached SDAI remission less often (adjusted HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.57–0.87) [Abuhelwa2021].

Sertraline, an SSRI, had no effect on synovitis, cartilage damage or bone erosion in CIA rats, unlike methotrexate and the biologics tested in the same study [Chmielewski2026]. For valproate, only its proposed target has been studied: two selective histone deacetylase 6 (HDAC6) inhibitors lowered arthritis scores in CIA mice [Oh2017], but we found no study that tested valproate itself in arthritis. We found no arthritis study of amitriptyline either.

In a nested case-control study of diabetic patients (790 RA cases and 11,850 controls), DPP-4 inhibitor use was not linked to RA after adjustment (adjusted OR 1.16, 95% CI 0.94–1.43) [Kathe2018]. In patients who did not use insulin, the odds were higher (OR 1.30, 95% CI 1.001–1.985) [Kathe2018]. We found no RA-specific study of allopurinol. For now, these drugs remain hypotheses or have mixed results, and they need well-designed animal studies before any trial in patients with RA.
