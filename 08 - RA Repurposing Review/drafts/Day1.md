## 4.1.1. Metformin
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1a-Metformin]] · 🎯 ~400 كلمة
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده: 
Metformin is a cheap first-line drug for type 2 diabetes, and it is generally safe. Its main mechanism is activation of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), which regulates glucose and lipid metabolism [Son2014]. Metformin also has anti-inflammatory effects [Son2014]. AMPK activation suppresses mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), and this modulates T-cell activity. For example, metformin inhibits the differentiation of Th17 cells, which are important drivers of chronic inflammation [Son2014].

In a preclinical collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) model, arthritic mice were given 5 mg/mouse of metformin orally three times per week for 9 weeks. This treatment significantly reduced arthritis severity and restored the Th17/Treg balance: Th17 activity fell and Treg activity rose, with less STAT3 and more STAT5 phosphorylation. In the same model, the drug also suppressed osteoclastogenic activity [Son2014].

In a different model in which CIA rats were given intraperitoneal (i.p.) injections of metformin 100 mg/kg every 3 days, metformin protected the knee joints on micro-computed tomography (micro-CT) and on histopathology. Micro-CT showed that metformin protected the trabecular bone, while histology showed reduced osteoclast formation and synovitis. Metformin also reduced systemic inflammation, chondrocyte apoptosis and cartilage matrix degradation [Fan2020].

In joint samples from patients with RA, AMPK-α1 levels were higher than in samples from osteoarthritis patients, and its active (phosphorylated) form was linked to higher TNF-α and higher scores on the 28-joint Disease Activity Score (DAS28). In cultured fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) from these patients, metformin slowed cell growth and movement, and the cells released less IL-6 and IL-1β, as well as less TNF-α [Chen2020].

A randomized, single-blind clinical trial enrolled 66 Egyptian patients with moderately or highly active RA. Of these, 33 patients received metformin 850 mg twice daily in addition to csDMARDs, and the other 33 patients received placebo. This led to reduced C-reactive protein (CRP) levels and DAS28-CRP scores after 6 months compared with placebo, and only three patients withdrew from the metformin group because of gastrointestinal side effects [Gharib2021]. In a prospective cohort study, 124 patients received metformin in addition to traditional csDMARD therapy for 90 days and were compared with 98 patients receiving csDMARDs only. This was associated with a faster fall in IL-6, TNF-α, HMGB1 and CRP than in the csDMARD-only group [Zhang2023].

In a large UK retrospective cohort of 224,551 patients with type 2 diabetes, metformin users showed no reduction in RA incidence [Zemedikun2021]. However, a Mendelian randomization study suggested that metformin may protect against RA [Liang2023]. Metformin may therefore be a safe and cheap add-on to csDMARDs to lower RA disease activity. Whether it prevents RA is still unclear, and larger and longer double-blind randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that also check joint damage on X-ray are needed.



## 4.2.1. Statins
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.2a-Statins]] · 🎯 ~450 كلمة
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:
HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins) are widely used in cardiovascular therapy. Statins act mainly by competitively inhibiting the HMG-CoA reductase enzyme, and this reduces de novo cholesterol synthesis [Ren2023]. Statins also have effects other than lowering lipids; for example, by lowering isoprenoid synthesis, they reduce the isoprenylation of Ras and Rho, which are small GTP-binding proteins [Chodick2010][ElBarbary2010]. Statins suppress interferon-γ-induced MHC class II expression, and this reduces T-cell activation [Chodick2010]. Atorvastatin has been reported to reduce several proinflammatory mediators, such as IL-6 and TNF-α, the chemokine CCL2, and the NF-κB pathway [Sarabi2016]. Both effects matter in RA, where T cells, IL-6 and TNF-α drive joint inflammation and damage [Xing2015][Sarabi2016]. Patients with RA also carry a higher cardiovascular risk, so statins may give them an extra benefit for the heart [Kitas2019].

In a preclinical study, mice with serum-induced arthritis were given atorvastatin and pravastatin at an intravenous dose of 0.2 mg/kg each. Mice that were administered statins had reduced paw swelling and less knee destruction on histopathology [Walker2017].

Several earlier studies in CIA mice concluded that statins reduced arthritis [Vandebriel2012]. However, when Vandebriel et al. gave oral statins to CIA mice, they did not see improvement; instead, both atorvastatin and pravastatin accelerated disease onset [Vandebriel2012].

In patients, two meta-analyses showed that statins reduced disease activity, as measured by DAS28 (standardized mean difference [SMD] −0.55 and −0.79) [Xing2015][Ren2023]. However, the included trials were small and mostly conducted at single centers [Xing2015], and both meta-analyses reported high heterogeneity [Xing2015][Ren2023]. In an unblinded randomized study conducted in Egypt, 30 patients with early RA were divided into two groups. Group 1 received conventional RA therapy with MTX and prednisone, while group 2 received MTX + prednisone + atorvastatin. DAS28 was significantly reduced from 6.09 to 3.9 in group 2 compared with 6.19 to 5.27 in group 1 [ElBarbary2010]. In contrast, the largest trial, TRACE RA, conducted in the UK on 3,002 patients, found no difference in DAS28 between patients who received atorvastatin and those who received placebo [Kitas2019]. Atorvastatin did lower CRP, but the authors judged this fall too small to matter clinically in RA [Kitas2019]. In short, statins lowered RA disease activity in small trials, but the largest trial did not confirm this. For now, their clearest benefit in RA is cheap and safe protection of the heart, and trials with disease activity as the main end point are needed before statins can be used to treat RA itself.
