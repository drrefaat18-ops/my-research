## 4.1. Antidiabetic Drugs

Several drugs for type 2 diabetes also act on pathways that drive inflammation in RA, such as AMPK and NF-κB (Section 3). Insulin resistance is also common in RA and is linked to higher disease activity [Ormseth2013]. This section first covers metformin and pioglitazone, followed by glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) and sodium–glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors.

## 4.1.1. Metformin
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1a-Metformin]] · 🎯 ≥400 كلمة (حد أدنى)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده: 
Metformin is an inexpensive first-line drug for type 2 diabetes and is generally safe [Gharib2021]. The drug appears to act mainly by activating AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), which in hepatocytes lowers acetyl-CoA carboxylase activity, increases fatty acid oxidation and suppresses lipogenic enzyme expression [Zhou2001]. Activated AMPK phosphorylates raptor, a step needed to inhibit mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) complex 1 (mTORC1) under energy stress [Gwinn2008]. Both mTOR complexes, mTORC1 and mTORC2, promote the differentiation of T helper 17 (Th17) cells [Nagai2013].

Animal studies consistently show that metformin reduces arthritis and protects the joint. In the collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) model (Table 1), immunized mice were given 5 mg/mouse of metformin orally three times per week for 9 weeks, starting 7 days after the first immunization. This treatment significantly reduced arthritis severity and shifted the Th17 to regulatory T (Treg) cell ratio, with fewer Th17 cells and more Treg cells. Phosphorylation of signal transducers and activators of transcription 3 and 5 (STAT3 and STAT5) fell and rose, respectively, and osteoclast activity was reduced [Son2014]. Similarly, in rats with CIA, intraperitoneal (i.p.) metformin (100 mg/kg every 3 days for 3 weeks) protected the knee joints on micro-computed tomography (micro-CT) and on histopathology. Micro-CT showed preserved trabecular bone, histology showed less osteoclast formation and synovitis, and metformin also lowered markers of systemic inflammation, chondrocyte apoptosis and cartilage matrix degradation [Fan2020]. After body-surface-area conversion for a 60-kg adult [NairJacob2016], the oral dose of 5 mg/mouse three times weekly [Son2014] equals approximately 970–1,220 mg per dose, assuming a 20–25-g mouse because body weight was not reported. The intraperitoneal dose of 100 mg/kg every 3 days in rats [Fan2020] equals approximately 970 mg per dose, and both values lie within the usual clinical range of 500–2,550 mg/day [Naffaa2020].

Data from human joint tissue also suggest a link between AMPK and RA activity. In synovium from patients with RA, the active (phosphorylated) form of AMPK-α1 was higher than in synovium from osteoarthritis patients; in their serum, phosphorylated AMPK-α1 rose with the 28-joint Disease Activity Score (DAS28), and total AMPK-α1 rose with tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) [Chen2020]. When fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) from these patients were cultured, metformin at high (millimolar) doses inhibited cell proliferation and migration and reduced TNF-α, interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-6, although the authors questioned whether such doses can be reached in patients [Chen2020].

Three clinical studies suggest a benefit of metformin as an add-on to conventional synthetic (cs) disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs). The largest randomized trial, a double-blind study from Egypt, assigned 120 patients with active RA to metformin 1000 mg or placebo daily, each added to methotrexate 7.5 mg/week, over 12 weeks [Abdallah2021]. At 12 weeks, 80.8% of the metformin group reached an American College of Rheumatology 20% improvement (ACR20) response, compared with 54.7% of the placebo group (P = 0.001), without serious adverse effects in either group [Abdallah2021]. A randomized, single-blind trial enrolled 66 Egyptian patients with moderately or highly active RA, of whom 33 received metformin 850 mg twice daily in addition to csDMARDs and 33 received placebo. After 6 months, C-reactive protein (CRP) levels and DAS28-CRP scores were lower than with placebo, and only three metformin-treated patients withdrew because of gastrointestinal side effects [Gharib2021]. In a non-randomized prospective cohort study, 124 patients received low-dose metformin (250 mg twice daily) in addition to traditional csDMARD therapy for 90 days and were compared with 98 patients receiving csDMARDs only. Metformin use was associated with a faster fall in IL-6, TNF-α, high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) and CRP than in the csDMARD-only group [Zhang2023]. A meta-analysis of four randomized trials with 350 patients found that metformin lowered CRP after 3 months (mean difference −5.99 mg/L, 95% CI from −7.46 to −4.52) [Alshammari2026]. Over the same period, DAS28 also fell in the three trials that reported it (mean difference −0.80, 95% CI from −0.87 to −0.73) [Alshammari2026]. However, each of the four trials was conducted in Egypt or India, and two carried a high risk of bias because they were single-blind and did not report trial registration [Alshammari2026].

Data on RA prevention are conflicting. In a large UK cohort of people recently diagnosed with type 2 diabetes, RA risk was the same in metformin users as in users of other oral diabetes drugs (hazard ratio [HR] after adjustment 1.00, 95% CI 0.82 to 1.22) [Zemedikun2021]. A Mendelian randomization study suggested that metformin may protect against RA, but its genetic markers reflect metformin use and therefore largely track diabetes [Liang2023]. In an Israeli cohort of 113,749 people starting metformin, adherence was associated with a lower RA risk, which was lowest when 40–59% of days were covered rather than under 20% (adjusted HR 0.62, 95% CI from 0.45 to 0.84) [Naffaa2020]. In analyses stratified by sex, this association was limited to women [Naffaa2020]. Therefore, metformin may be a safe and inexpensive add-on to csDMARDs to lower RA disease activity. Whether it prevents RA is still unclear, and larger and longer double-blind randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that also check joint damage on X-ray are needed.



## 4.2. Lipid-Lowering Drugs

Because patients with RA have a higher cardiovascular risk, lipid-lowering drugs are a logical choice to test [Kitas2019]. Statins and fenofibrate, the two classes covered in this section, also act on immune cells and may therefore benefit the joints as well as the heart.

## 4.2.1. Statins
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.2a-Statins]] · 🎯 ≥450 كلمة (حد أدنى)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:
Statins are widely used in cardiovascular therapy and act mainly as competitive inhibitors of 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA reductase (HMG-CoA reductase), which reduces de novo cholesterol synthesis [Ren2023]. Statins also have effects other than lowering lipids: by reducing isoprenoid synthesis, they limit the isoprenylation of small guanosine triphosphate (GTP)-binding proteins such as Ras and Rho [Chodick2010][ElBarbary2010]. They also suppress interferon-γ-induced major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class II expression, which reduces T-cell activation [Chodick2010], a key driver of joint inflammation in RA [Xing2015].

Animal studies of statins are mostly positive, but not all. In a preclinical study, mice with serum-induced arthritis were given atorvastatin, pravastatin or simvastatin intravenously (0.2 mg/kg on days 3, 5 and 7). Atorvastatin and pravastatin reduced paw swelling and knee destruction on histopathology, but simvastatin had no significant effect [Walker2017].

As Vandebriel et al. point out, most earlier CIA studies, which often used high intraperitoneal doses, found statins protective [Vandebriel2012]. However, when they gave CIA mice oral atorvastatin at 0.2 mg/day or oral pravastatin at 0.8 mg/day, arthritis started earlier, and every treated mouse became arthritic, against 7 of 12 controls [Vandebriel2012]. To our knowledge, no other group has reported harm [Vandebriel2012].

Results in patients are mixed. Two meta-analyses showed that statins reduced disease activity measured by DAS28, with standardized mean differences (SMD) of −0.55 (95% CI −0.83 to −0.26) and −0.78 (95% CI −1.08 to −0.49) [Xing2015][Ren2023]. However, the included trials were small and mostly conducted at single centers [Xing2015], and both meta-analyses reported high heterogeneity [Xing2015][Ren2023]. In an unblinded randomized study conducted in Egypt, 30 patients with early RA were divided into two groups of 15 and treated for 6 months with MTX and prednisone, either alone (group 1) or with atorvastatin (group 2). DAS28 was significantly reduced from 6.09 to 3.9 in group 2 compared with 6.19 to 5.27 in group 1 [ElBarbary2010]. In contrast, the largest trial, the double-blind Trial of Atorvastatin for primary prevention of Cardiovascular Events in RA (TRACE RA), enrolled 3,002 UK patients for cardiovascular end points and found no difference in DAS28 between patients who received atorvastatin and those who received placebo [Kitas2019]. Atorvastatin did lower CRP, but the authors judged this fall too small to matter clinically in RA [Kitas2019]. In short, statins appeared to lower RA disease activity in small trials, but the largest trial did not confirm this. For now, statins in RA are used to lower lipids and cardiovascular risk, as in other patients, and trials with disease activity as the main end point are needed before they can be used to treat RA itself.

## 4.2.2. Fenofibrate
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1b-PPAR-agonists]] · 🎯 ≥150 كلمة (حد أدنى)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:

Fenofibrate activates PPAR-α and is mainly used to treat high triglycerides [Zhou2012]. In mouse T cells, fenofibrate (5–20 μM) inhibited Th17-cell differentiation in a dose-dependent manner, by 74% at 20 μM, whereas other PPAR-α agonists (WY14643, GW7647, and bezafibrate) had no such effect, which suggests that fenofibrate acts here through another pathway [Zhou2012]. In AIA rats, fenofibrate (300 mg/kg/day for 12 days) lowered the arthritis score from 11.0 to 5.83, a fall of about 47% (P < 0.01) [Castillero2012]. Clinical data on fenofibrate in RA are very limited, and to our knowledge no placebo-controlled trial has yet tested whether the effects seen in animals also benefit patients with RA.
