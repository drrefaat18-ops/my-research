## 4.1.1. Metformin
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1a-Metformin]] · 🎯 ~400 كلمة
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده: 
Metformin is a cheap anti-diabetic drug used for type 2 diabetes, and it is generally safe. Its main mechanism is AMPK activation, which regulates glucose and lipid metabolism. Metformin also has an anti-inflammatory effect. AMPK activation suppresses mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), and this modulates T-cell activity. For example, metformin inhibits the differentiation of Th17 cells, which are important drivers of chronic inflammation [Son2014].

In a pre-clinical collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) experiment, arthritic mice were given 5 mg/mouse of metformin orally 3 times per week for 9 weeks. The resulting effect was a significant reduction of arthritis severity, reduced Th17 activity, and increased Treg activity through reduced STAT3 and increased STAT5 phosphorylation, so the Th17/Treg balance moved back toward normal. In the same model, the drug also suppressed osteoclastogenic activity [Son2014].

In a different model in which CIA rats were given intraperitoneal (i.p.) injections of metformin 100 mg/kg every 3 days, metformin protected the joints, as examined by micro-computed tomography (micro-CT) and histopathological analysis of rat knee joints. Micro-CT showed that metformin protected the trabecular bone, and it also reduced osteoclast formation. The knee joints showed reduced synovitis. Metformin also reduced systemic inflammation, chondrocyte apoptosis and cartilage matrix degradation [Fan2020].

In joint samples from RA patients, AMPK-α1 levels were higher than in samples from osteoarthritis patients, and its active (phosphorylated) form was linked to higher DAS28 and CRP. In cultured fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) from these patients, metformin slowed cell growth and movement, and the cells released less IL-6 and IL-1β, as well as less TNF-α [Chen2020].

In a randomized, single-blinded clinical trial, a total of 66 Egyptian patients with moderately or highly active rheumatoid arthritis were enrolled. Of these, 33 patients received metformin 850 mg twice daily in addition to csDMARDs, and the other 33 patients received placebo. This led to reduced CRP levels and DAS28-CRP scores after 6 months compared to placebo, and only 3 patients withdrew from the metformin group because of GI side effects [Gharib2021]. In another prospective cohort study, 124 patients received metformin in addition to traditional csDMARD therapy for 90 days and were compared with 98 patients receiving csDMARDs only. This was associated with a faster fall in IL-6, TNF-α, HMGB1 and CRP than in the csDMARD-only group [Zhang2023]. In a larger UK study of type 2 diabetes mellitus patients, metformin use did not reduce the risk of developing RA [Zemedikun2021]. However, a Mendelian randomization study suggested that metformin may protect against rheumatoid arthritis [Liang2023]. Metformin may be a safe, affordable add-on to csDMARDs for reducing RA disease activity. However, its role in preventing RA remains uncertain, and larger, longer double-blind RCTs with radiographic outcomes are needed to confirm its benefit.
