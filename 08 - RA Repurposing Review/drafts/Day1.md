## 4.1. Antidiabetic Drugs

Several drugs for type 2 diabetes also act on pathways that drive inflammation in RA, such as AMPK and NF-κB (Section 3). Insulin resistance is also common in RA and is linked to higher disease activity [Ormseth2013]. It covers metformin and pioglitazone first, then the newer GLP-1 receptor agonists, and finally SGLT2 inhibitors.

## 4.1.1. Metformin
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1a-Metformin]] · 🎯 ~400 كلمة
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده: 
Metformin is a cheap first-line drug for type 2 diabetes, and it is generally safe [Gharib2021]. Its main mechanism is activation of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), which regulates glucose and lipid metabolism [Son2014]. Metformin also has anti-inflammatory effects [Son2014]. AMPK activation suppresses mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), and this changes how T cells behave. For example, metformin inhibits the differentiation of Th17 cells, which are important drivers of chronic inflammation [Son2014].

Animal studies consistently show that metformin reduces arthritis and protects the joint. In the collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) model (Table 1), immunized mice were given 5 mg/mouse of metformin orally three times per week for 9 weeks, starting 7 days after the first immunization. This treatment significantly reduced arthritis severity and restored the Th17/Treg balance: Th17 cells fell and Treg cells rose, with less STAT3 and more STAT5 phosphorylation. In the same model, the drug also reduced osteoclast activity [Son2014]. In rats with CIA, intraperitoneal (i.p.) metformin (100 mg/kg every 3 days for 3 weeks) protected the knee joints on micro-computed tomography (micro-CT) and on histopathology. Micro-CT showed that metformin protected the trabecular bone, while histology showed reduced osteoclast formation and synovitis. Metformin also reduced systemic inflammation, chondrocyte apoptosis and cartilage matrix degradation [Fan2020].

Human joint tissue also links AMPK to RA activity. In synovium from patients with RA, the active (phosphorylated) form of AMPK-α1 was higher than in synovium from osteoarthritis patients. In their serum, phosphorylated AMPK-α1 rose with the 28-joint Disease Activity Score (DAS28), and total AMPK-α1 rose with TNF-α [Chen2020]. When fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) from these patients were cultured, metformin at high (millimolar) doses slowed cell growth and movement and reduced IL-1β and IL-6 as well as TNF-α [Chen2020]. The authors doubted that such doses can be reached in patients [Chen2020].

Two small clinical studies support adding metformin to csDMARDs. A randomized, single-blind clinical trial enrolled 66 Egyptian patients with moderately or highly active RA. Of these, 33 patients received metformin 850 mg twice daily in addition to csDMARDs, and the other 33 patients received placebo. This led to reduced C-reactive protein (CRP) levels and DAS28-CRP scores after 6 months compared with placebo, and only three patients withdrew from the metformin group because of gastrointestinal side effects [Gharib2021]. In a non-randomized prospective cohort study, 124 patients received low-dose metformin (250 mg twice daily) in addition to traditional csDMARD therapy for 90 days and were compared with 98 patients receiving csDMARDs only. This was associated with a faster fall in IL-6, TNF-α, HMGB1 and CRP than in the csDMARD-only group [Zhang2023].

Data on RA prevention are conflicting. A large UK cohort followed people recently diagnosed with type 2 diabetes. RA risk was the same in metformin users as in users of other oral diabetes drugs (hazard ratio [HR] after adjustment 1.00, 95% CI 0.82 to 1.22) [Zemedikun2021]. A Mendelian randomization study suggested that metformin may protect against RA, but its genetic markers reflect metformin use and so mostly track diabetes [Liang2023]. Metformin may therefore be a safe and cheap add-on to csDMARDs to lower RA disease activity. Whether it prevents RA is still unclear, and larger and longer double-blind randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that also check joint damage on X-ray are needed.



## 4.2. Lipid-Lowering Drugs

Patients with RA have a higher cardiovascular risk, so lipid-lowering drugs are a natural choice to test [Kitas2019]. Statins and fenofibrate also act on immune cells, so they may help the joints as well as the heart. This section covers both.

## 4.2.1. Statins
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.2a-Statins]] · 🎯 ~450 كلمة
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:
HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins) are widely used in cardiovascular therapy. Statins act mainly by competitively inhibiting the HMG-CoA reductase enzyme, and this reduces de novo cholesterol synthesis [Ren2023]. Statins also have effects other than lowering lipids. For example, they make less isoprenoid, so small GTP-binding proteins such as Ras and Rho are less isoprenylated [Chodick2010][ElBarbary2010]. Statins suppress interferon-γ-induced MHC class II expression, and this reduces T-cell activation [Chodick2010]. This matters in RA, where T-cell activation drives joint inflammation [Xing2015].

Animal studies of statins are mostly positive, but not all. In a preclinical study, mice with serum-induced arthritis were given atorvastatin, pravastatin or simvastatin intravenously (0.2 mg/kg on days 3, 5 and 7). Atorvastatin and pravastatin reduced paw swelling and knee destruction on histopathology, but simvastatin had no significant effect [Walker2017].

As Vandebriel et al. point out, most earlier CIA studies, which often used high intraperitoneal doses, found statins protective [Vandebriel2012]. However, when they gave CIA mice oral atorvastatin at 0.2 mg/day or oral pravastatin at 0.8 mg/day, arthritis started earlier, and every treated mouse became arthritic, against 7 of 12 controls [Vandebriel2012]. They are the only group so far to report harm [Vandebriel2012].

Results in patients are mixed. Two meta-analyses showed that statins reduced disease activity, as measured by DAS28 (standardized mean difference [SMD] −0.55, 95% CI −0.83 to −0.26, and −0.78, 95% CI −1.08 to −0.49) [Xing2015][Ren2023]. However, the included trials were small and mostly conducted at single centers [Xing2015], and both meta-analyses reported high heterogeneity [Xing2015][Ren2023]. In an unblinded randomized study conducted in Egypt, 30 patients with early RA were divided into two groups of 15 and treated for 6 months. Group 1 received conventional RA therapy with MTX and prednisone, while group 2 received MTX + prednisone + atorvastatin. DAS28 was significantly reduced from 6.09 to 3.9 in group 2 compared with 6.19 to 5.27 in group 1 [ElBarbary2010]. In contrast, the largest trial, TRACE RA, a double-blind trial in 3,002 UK patients designed for cardiovascular end points, found no difference in DAS28 between patients who received atorvastatin and those who received placebo [Kitas2019]. Atorvastatin did lower CRP, but the authors judged this fall too small to matter clinically in RA [Kitas2019]. In short, statins lowered RA disease activity in small trials, but the largest trial did not confirm this. For now, statins in RA are used to lower lipids and cardiovascular risk, as in other patients. Trials with disease activity as the main end point are needed before statins can be used to treat RA itself.

## 4.2.2. Fenofibrate
> 📖 الإملاء: [[4.1b-PPAR-agonists]] · 🎯 ~150 كلمة
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:

Fenofibrate activates PPAR-α and is mainly used to treat high triglycerides [Zhou2012]. In mouse T cells, fenofibrate (5–20 μM) blocked Th17-cell differentiation more as the dose rose, by 74% at 20 μM [Zhou2012]. Other PPAR-α agonists (WY14643, GW7647, and bezafibrate) had no such effect, so fenofibrate may act here through another pathway [Zhou2012]. In AIA rats, fenofibrate (300 mg/kg/day for 12 days) lowered the arthritis score from 11.0 to 5.83, a fall of about 47% (P < 0.01) [Castillero2012]. Clinical data on fenofibrate in RA are very limited. No placebo-controlled trial has yet tested whether the effects seen in animals also help patients with RA.
