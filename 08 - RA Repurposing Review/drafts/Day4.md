## 3. Pathophysiology of RA and Druggable Pathways
> 📖 الإملاء: [[3-Pathophysiology]] · 🎯 ≥600 كلمة (حد أدنى)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:

Chronic systemic inflammation in rheumatoid arthritis (RA), an autoimmune disease, progressively damages the joints and can cause lifelong disability [Kondo2021]. A network of cytokines and cells promotes synovial cell proliferation and injures cartilage and bone [Kondo2021]. In a stepwise process, dendritic cells (DCs) recruit and activate T cells, which then stimulate osteoclasts, chondrocytes, synoviocytes, macrophages and B cells to secrete bone-destroying and pro-inflammatory mediators [Ding2023]. They include enzymes, the matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), and three cytokines: tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), IL-1β and interleukin (IL)-6 [Ding2023]. Fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) become aggressive, tumor-like cells instead of harmless ones [Ding2023]. TNF-α and IL-6 are central cytokines in RA pathogenesis [Kondo2021]. IL-17, IL-21, IL-23 and IL-1β also contribute [Kondo2021]. A TNF superfamily cytokine, the ligand of receptor activator of nuclear factor-κB (RANKL), mainly drives osteoclast differentiation [Kondo2021]. Antagonists of TNF-α and the IL-6 receptor (IL-6R) have been used successfully in the clinic, which shows that cytokine-targeted therapy is feasible in RA [Gao2024]. Better understanding of RA pathology has also produced biological disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (bDMARDs) and Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors [Kondo2021].

Drug discovery often targets abnormal intracellular signals [Ding2023]. Pathways involved in RA pathogenesis include JAK/signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT), phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (AKT), mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), WNT and spleen tyrosine kinase (SYK) signaling [Ding2023]. When nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) is activated, it raises the output of TNF-α as well as IL-1β and IL-6, and speeds RA progression [Ding2023]. Signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) signaling, triggered by IL-23, IL-6 and IL-21, is the main route by which T helper 17 (Th17) cells differentiate [Kondo2021]. The PI3K/AKT pathway is an intracellular signaling route that regulates cell survival, angiogenesis, metabolism and proliferation in response to external signals [Ding2023]. Inhibiting any pathway except JAK/STAT is considered difficult because of crosstalk, in which signals reach a pathway from other routes [Kondo2021]. However, targeting the JAK/STAT pathway has reached the clinic: the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) of the United States has approved some JAK inhibitors, and baricitinib, a selective JAK1/2 inhibitor, is also approved for RA (Figure 1) [Ding2023; Gao2024].

Amino acid metabolism, fatty acid synthesis, fatty acid oxidation, the pentose phosphate pathway, the tricarboxylic acid cycle and glycolysis are six major pathways with important roles in several aspects of RA progression [Gao2024]. In RA, CD4+ T cells shift from glycolysis toward the pentose phosphate pathway, whereas FLS increase anaerobic glycolysis and produce excess lactate [Gao2024]. Expression of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR)-γ was reduced and that of TNF-α increased in human RA FLS; in TNF-α-induced FLS in vitro, pioglitazone, rosiglitazone or PPAR-γ overexpression lowered IL-8, IL-6 and IL-1β and raised IL-10, and PPAR-γ was associated with p53 [LiXF2023]. Metformin activated AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) in hepatocytes, and AMPK must phosphorylate raptor for mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) complex 1 (mTORC1) to be inhibited under energy stress [Zhou2001; Gwinn2008]. Pioglitazone was reported to activate PPAR-γ and promote mitophagic flux, which restrained aberrant inflammasome activation and IL-18 and IL-1β release [Fu2026]. In a Th17 differentiation system, fenofibrate lowered both STAT3 activation and IL-21 output, and its inhibition of Th17 differentiation might not require PPAR-α [Zhou2012]. When interferon-γ was used to induce class II major histocompatibility complex molecules, statins reduced this induction, and because L-mevalonate abolished this effect, it reflects their inhibition of 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl coenzyme A reductase [Kwak2000]. Losartan, a blocker of the angiotensin II type 1 receptor, ameliorated arthritis in an experimental model, and in cultured RA synovial cells, RANKL upregulation by angiotensin II was mitigated by type 1 receptor blockade but not by type 2 receptor blockade [Wang2013; Zhao2021]. Dapagliflozin, a sodium–glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor, increased the ratio of phosphorylated to total AMPK in the joints of rats with adjuvant arthritis [ElDemerdash2025]. In FLS in vitro, the beneficial effects of dulaglutide, a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist, were linked to c-Jun N-terminal kinase/NF-κB signaling [Zheng2019]. This mechanistic evidence derives largely from cell-based and animal experiments rather than from studies in patients. Endothelial dysfunction, altered lipid profiles, elevated oxidative stress and systemic inflammation may contribute to a higher cardiovascular risk in patients with RA or gout [Hansildaar2021]. Drugs that act on cellular metabolism and on cardiometabolic risk may therefore be able to address joint inflammation and its comorbidities together, a possibility examined in Section 4.


## 2. Methods
> 📖 الإملاء: [[2-Methods]] · 🎯 ≥200 كلمة (حد أدنى)
> ✏️ اكتب تحت السطر ده:

For this narrative review, we searched one database, Europe PubMed Central (Europe PMC). Eight searches were run in the title and abstract fields, one per drug group: statins, angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), fenofibrate, metformin, pioglitazone, GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RA), SGLT2 inhibitors and DPP-4 inhibitors. Each search, listed in full in Supplementary Table S1, paired the drug terms with "rheumatoid arthritis", "collagen-induced arthritis", "adjuvant-induced arthritis" or "inflammatory arthritis", and class terms such as thiazolidinedione and fibrate were added for most groups, whereas metformin was searched by name only. The searches were run on 6 October 2026 and repeated on 11 October 2026. Our description of the search covers item 3 of the Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles (SANRA), which is an appraisal scale for narrative reviews rather than a guideline for reporting them [Baethge2019].

Records indexed as reviews were filtered out, but no date or language limit was set. Where two studies answered the same question, the more recent one (2022 onward) was preferred. Every included article was in English, and full texts came from open access or the Egyptian Knowledge Bank (EKB). Only the abstract was used for five studies, and two trials were known only from their registry records. Narrative reviews found outside the search supplied background and possible mechanisms, whereas three meta-analyses, one on metformin and two on statins, supplied pooled results. Among eligible records, randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and large cohort studies came first, then mechanistic animal studies. Because reasons for exclusion were not recorded at screening, this step may not be fully reproducible.

The eight searches returned 628 records on both runs (statins 384, metformin 79, pioglitazone 65, ARBs 40, fenofibrate 22, DPP-4 inhibitors 17, GLP-1 RA 12 and SGLT2 inhibitors 9), and removing 24 duplicates left 604 records for screening. Of these, 48 were included and 556 were not. In addition, 31 references came from outside the search (primary studies missed by the search terms, narrative reviews, meta-analyses, drug labels, trial registry records and two methods papers), and 79 were cited overall (Figure 4). Because only one database was searched, eligible studies indexed elsewhere may have been missed.
