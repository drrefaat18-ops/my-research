## Abstract


#### Background

Difficult-to-treat rheumatoid arthritis (D2T RA) is a clinically challenging condition characterized by persistent disease activity despite appropriate disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) therapy. Evidence regarding its frequency and contributing factors in Saudi Arabia remains limited.


#### Objective

To determine the proportion of patients with D2T RA and identify the demographic, clinical, laboratory, and treatment-related factors associated with it in a multicentre Saudi cohort.


#### Methods

This multicentre cross-sectional study used data from a registry of 928 patients with RA attending tertiary centres in Saudi Arabia. Patients were classified according to the EULAR definition as having D2T RA or controlled RA, defined as remission or low disease activity. Clinical, laboratory, and treatment-related characteristics were compared, and multivariable logistic regression identified factors independently associated with D2T RA.

## Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic systemic autoimmune disease characterized by persistent synovial inflammation, progressive joint destruction, functional disability, and impaired quality of life. Advances in therapeutic strategies, including conventional synthetic, biological, and targeted synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs), have substantially improved clinical outcomes, making remission or low disease activity achievable for many patients.1

Nevertheless, a clinically important subgroup continues to experience persistent disease activity or RA-related symptoms despite multiple therapeutic interventions. To standardize this heterogeneous condition, the European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR) introduced a formal definition of difficult-to-treat rheumatoid arthritis (D2T RA), incorporating multiple DMARD failure, evidence of active or progressive disease, and the perception by the rheumatologist and/or patient that disease management remains problematic.2

D2T RA is influenced by both inflammatory and non-inflammatory factors. Previous studies have reported associations with persistent inflammatory activity, functional disability, comorbidities, socioeconomic disadvantage, treatment-related adverse effects, non-adherence, discrepancies between patient and physician treatment goals, and concomitant fibromyalgia.3 These factors contribute to greater disease burden and may complicate the distinction between pharmacologically refractory inflammation and persistent symptoms driven by non-inflammatory mechanisms.

Real-world cohorts and a recent meta-analysis have demonstrated substantial variation in the reported frequency of D2T RA across different populations and healthcare settings.4–6 Differences in patient selection, eligibility criteria, disease duration, treatment exposure, referral patterns, and access to advanced therapies may contribute to this variation.

Evidence regarding D2T RA in Saudi Arabia remains limited. Regional differences in referral pathways, access to specialist rheumatology services and advanced therapies, socioeconomic characteristics, health literacy, treatment adherence, and the concentration of complex cases within tertiary centres may influence both the observed proportion and clinical profile of D2T RA. Evaluating this condition within a multicentre Saudi registry may therefore provide context-specific evidence to support earlier recognition and individualized disease management.

Accordingly, this study aimed to determine the proportion of patients fulfilling the EULAR definition of D2T RA within a selected multicentre cohort of patients with established RA in Saudi Arabia and to identify the demographic, clinical, laboratory, and treatment-related factors associated with D2T RA.


## Methods


### Study Design and Population

This multicentre cross-sectional study used data from the Saudi Rheumatoid Arthritis Registry, which comprised 928 patients with complete clinical records followed at tertiary rheumatology centres. Eligibility for the source registry required at least three years of follow-up and a minimum of two documented rheumatology visits annually.

The registry represented the full spectrum of RA disease states. Of the 928 patients, 126 fulfilled the EULAR definition of D2T RA, representing 13.6% of the source registry. For the comparative analysis, 269 patients in remission or with low disease activity were included as the controlled RA group. The remaining 533 patients, who had intermediate disease activity and did not fulfil the D2T RA definition, were excluded from the between-group analysis. The final comparative analytical cohort therefore comprised 395 patients: 126 with D2T RA and 269 with controlled RA (Figure 1). ### Definition and Classification of the Study Groups

D2T RA was operationalized according to the EULAR definition.2 Patients were classified as having D2T RA only when all three mandatory domains were fulfilled. First, patients must have received treatment according to EULAR recommendations, with failure of conventional synthetic DMARD therapy, unless contraindicated, followed by failure of at least two biological or targeted synthetic DMARDs with different mechanisms of action.

Second, patients were required to have at least one of the following features of active or progressive disease: moderate or high disease activity according to a validated composite measure, including DAS28-ESR >3.2; signs or symptoms suggestive of active disease; inability to taper glucocorticoids below 7.5 mg/day of prednisone or equivalent; rapid radiographic progression, with or without other evidence of active disease; or persistent RA-related symptoms causing reduced quality of life despite apparently controlled inflammatory activity.

Third, management of the patient’s signs or symptoms had to be perceived as problematic by the rheumatologist and/or the patient, as documented in the registry or clinical records. Patients were classified as having D2T RA only when the treatment-failure domain, at least one active or symptomatic disease feature, and the problematic-management domain were all fulfilled.

Patients who did not fulfil the EULAR definition of D2T RA and had remission or low disease activity were classified as the controlled RA group. Exposure to biological or targeted synthetic DMARDs was not required for inclusion in the controlled group.


### Study Outcomes

The primary outcome was the proportion of patients fulfilling the EULAR definition of difficult-to-treat rheumatoid arthritis within the complete source registry patients.

Secondary outcomes included comparisons between D2T RA and controlled RA groups with respect to demographic characteristics, clinical features, laboratory findings, treatment patterns, and patient-reported outcomes, as well as the contribution of non-inflammatory factors such as fibromyalgia and treatment adherence.


### Participants

Patients included in this analysis were selected from the registry database using predefined inclusion criteria.

Adult patients aged ≥18 years with a rheumatologist-confirmed diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis according to the ACR/EULAR 2010 classification criteria7 were included. Eligible patients were assigned to the D2T RA or controlled RA group according to the definitions described above. Patients who did not meet the criteria for D2T RA and had low disease activity or remission8 were included as the control group.

The source registry had already applied its requirements for complete clinical records, at least three years of follow-up, and a minimum of two documented rheumatology visits annually. For the present comparative analysis, patients with intermediate disease activity who did not fulfil the EULAR D2T RA definition were excluded. Pregnancy, non-Saudi nationality, and overlap connective tissue disease were exclusion criteria applied according to the registry protocol.


### Assessment

All included patients underwent a comprehensive clinical, laboratory, and treatment-related assessment at baseline and during follow-up.

Demographic data included age at diagnosis, sex, educational level, occupation, smoking status, and family history of rheumatic diseases. Disease-related history included disease duration and the time from first joint symptoms to initial medical consultation. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated.

Clinical assessment included tender and swollen joint counts, joint deformities, morning stiffness, extra-articular manifestations, and comorbidities. Disease activity was assessed using the Disease Activity Score in 28 joints (DAS-28)8 at the first and last visits, as well as the Simplified Disease Activity Index (SDAI) and Clinical Disease Activity Index (CDAI).9

Functional disability was assessed using the Health Assessment Questionnaire (HAQ).10 Patient global assessment was recorded using a visual analogue scale (VAS), and fatigue was assessed as a patient-reported outcome. Fibromyalgia was recorded as a comorbid condition. Treatment adherence was categorized as adherent or non-adherent.

Laboratory investigations included ESR, CRP, RF, anti-CCP, ANA, and renal function tests. Additional screening included hepatitis markers, PPD, and antiphospholipid antibodies.

Treatment history included methotrexate, hydroxychloroquine, and glucocorticoid use.

The last available registry visit was defined as the index visit for the present analysis. D2T RA or controlled RA status was determined at the index visit using the patient’s cumulative treatment history and clinical status. Demographic characteristics were obtained at registry enrolment, while baseline DAS28-ESR and HAQ values were obtained from the first registry visit. Clinical disease activity, functional status, patient-reported outcomes, and inflammatory markers used in the between-group analysis were obtained at the index visit or from the assessment closest to that visit. Treatment exposure and DMARD failure were evaluated cumulatively from registry enrolment through the index visit.


### Ethical Considerations

This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Data were obtained from the Saudi Arthritis Registry (SAR), which was established following approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB), Prince Sultan Military Medical City (PSMMC), Saudi Arabia (Project No. 1306).

The present study entitled Patients with Difficult-To-Treat Rheumatoid Arthritis in Saudi Arabia received additional approval from the Institutional Review Board of Prince Sultan Military Medical City (Approval No. E-2422) as well as from the Research and Ethics Committee, Medical Services General Directorate, Western Region (Approval No. REC 2023–797; Registration No. H-02-T-078).

Written informed consent for participation and use of clinical data for research purposes was obtained from all participants at the time of registry enrollment. Patient data were anonymized prior to analysis to ensure confidentiality and privacy.


### Data Management and Statistical Analysis

Data were analysed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS), version 28 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was applied to assess the normality of distribution of quantitative variables. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation or median and range, as appropriate. Pearson’s chi-square test was used for categorical comparisons when expected cell-count assumptions were satisfied. Fisher’s exact test was used for sparse 2×2 contingency tables, whereas the Fisher–Freeman–Halton exact test with Monte Carlo estimation was applied to larger contingency tables with sparse expected cell counts. Continuous variables were compared using the independent-samples t-test or Mann–Whitney U-test, as appropriate. Binary logistic regression was used to identify factors independently associated with D2T-RA, with D2T-RA coded as 1 and controls as 0. Candidate variables were selected based on their clinical relevance and statistical significance in the univariate comparison analyses rather than according to a predefined univariable p-value threshold. All covariates were entered simultaneously into the multivariable logistic regression model using the Enter method; no forward or backward stepwise selection procedure was applied. Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factor (VIF) and tolerance; VIF <5 and tolerance >0.20 were considered acceptable. No variable was excluded because of multicollinearity. Model fit was assessed using the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. Biologic and targeted synthetic DMARD variables were not included because treatment failure is incorporated into the D2T-RA definition, and inclusion could introduce circularity. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Discussion

The problem of difficult-to-treat rheumatoid arthritis (D2T RA) emerges a clinically relevant subgroup due to persistent disease activity, despite exposure to various disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs).11 This multicentre study characterized D2T RA within a selected Saudi cohort of patients with established RA and prolonged follow-up. Among the 928 patients in the complete source registry, 126 (13.6%) fulfilled the EULAR definition of D2T RA. The comparative analysis included these 126 patients and 269 patients with controlled RA; D2T RA was associated with higher disease activity, greater functional disability, elevated ESR, poorer patient global assessment, lower treatment adherence, and more frequent joint deformities and swollen joints. These findings demonstrate the multifactorial nature of D2T RA, involving inflammatory disease activity, accumulated disease burden, and patient-related factors.12

D2T RA accounted for 13.6% of the complete source registry. This estimate was higher than the 7.9% reported in the KURAMA cohort,4 comparable to the 14.4% reported in the overall BRASS registry,5 and within the range summarized by the recent global meta-analysis, which reported a pooled prevalence of 11.7%.6 Differences in patient characteristics, disease duration, treatment exposure, referral patterns, healthcare settings, and access to advanced therapies may explain the variation between studies. The high proportion of female patients in the two groups is consistent with the established epidemiology of RA, but no significant gender difference was found between D2T and controlled patients. This finding is in line with the results of Roodenrijs NMT et al (2021), who showed that sex alone was not independently associated with D2T status. even though females have higher baseline prevalence of RA.3 Conversely, the current study found a significant association between D2T RA and younger age at the time of diagnosis (<49 years), which is consistent with the findings of Kearsley-Fleet L et al, who postulated that early-onset RA might be an indication of a more aggressive immunological phenotype that might require intensive treatment options.13

The reduced level of education and increased unemployment levels among D2T patients demonstrate the contribution of socioeconomic factors to the outcomes of the disease. These results align with the research findings like Putrik P et al (2015), which established that worse disease control is related to lower socioeconomic status, which may be mediated by delayed access to healthcare and decreased treatment adherence.14 Patients with D2T RA had a significantly longer interval between symptom onset and the first medical consultation. Although this finding may reflect delayed healthcare seeking or barriers to timely medical assessment, the study did not evaluate early symptom trajectories or the interval between the first consultation and definitive diagnosis. Therefore, it should not be interpreted as direct evidence of delayed diagnostic recognition. Although this variable was not independently associated with D2T RA in multivariable analysis, it may reflect delayed access to care, which has been previously linked to worse outcomes and increased disease burden.

D2T patients had significantly higher rates of joint deformities and swollen joints, while the number of tender joints did not differ significantly between groups, which may reflect the subjective nature of tender joint assessment and the potential influence of concomitant fibromyalgia. The close association between joint deformities and D2T status supports the view that irreversible structural damage may contribute to treatment difficulty and persistent disease burden.11 Yamada S et al also noted similar results and emphasized that cumulative joint damage was a characteristic feature of refractory RA.15 Baseline DAS-28 values were similar between groups, although the difference approached statistical significance (p = 0.05). Moreover, higher disease activity at the final visit (DAS-28) persists in D2T patients, indicates a poor response to therapy, which is in line with previous longitudinal cohorts.16,17

HAQ-based functional disability was significantly greater in D2T patients and remained independently associated with D2T status. This finding can be compared to the evidence provided by Buch MH et al (2021), which states that the higher the disability scores, the more refractory disease and worse long-term outcomes.18 On the same note, Patient-reported outcomes, including patient global assessment VAS scores, were worse in patients with D2T RA, indicating a greater disease burden.

Although RF and anti-CCP were more frequently positive in D2T patients, they were not independently associated with D2T RA in multivariable analysis. D2T patients showed higher ESR levels, supporting the presence of persistent inflammatory activity in this subgroup.19,20 The final model established ESR as an independent factor between D2T RA and persistent systemic inflammation which impacts this specific group of patients.21,22

The increased rate of renal impairment in D2T patients may reflect disease burden, but not independently associated D2T in multivariate adjustment, it could be due to cumulative disease burden, chronic inflammation, or medication-related toxicity, which has been previously reported in observational cohort studies.23,24 Although fibromyalgia was more prevalent among D2T patients, it was not independently associated with D2T RA in multivariable analysis, suggesting its role may be related to symptom perception rather than underlying disease refractoriness. This is in line with the results of Pollard LC et al, who showed that comorbid fibromyalgia may exaggerate the disease activity scores and make treatment choices difficult.25

A key finding of this study is the strong association between treatment non-adherence and D2T RA, which remained significant after multivariable adjustment. Non-adherence is increasingly recognized as a major contributor to suboptimal outcomes in RA and may result in unnecessary treatment escalation, including premature switching to advanced therapies. This finding is consistent with prior reports, including those by Roodenrijs NMT,26 which identify non-adherence as an important modifiable factor underlying apparent treatment failure. These observations highlight the need to distinguish true refractory inflammatory disease from suboptimal disease control driven by behavioural or healthcare system factors. The strength of this association in our cohort underscores the importance of systematically assessing and addressing adherence as a central component of D2T RA management.

Methotrexate use was more frequent among patients with D2T RA. These associations should not be interpreted as causal drug effects but may reflect treatment escalation, greater disease burden, and confounding by indication.27

In the present study, multivariable analysis identified several factors independently associated with D2T RA, including age at diagnosis, longer disease duration, higher disease activity (DAS-28), greater functional disability (HAQ), elevated ESR, poorer patient global assessment, non-adherence to treatment, and the use of methotrexate and prednisolone. These findings suggest that D2T RA reflects a combination of ongoing inflammatory activity, accumulated disease burden, patient-related factors, and treatment-related challenges.28

This study has several limitations. The cross-sectional design precludes assessment of temporal or causal relationships between identified factors and D2T RA. The restriction to patients with ≥3 years of follow-up may introduce selection bias and limit generalizability, potentially overrepresenting patients with more complex or established disease. In addition, adherence was assessed using routine clinical documentation, which may not fully capture its dynamic and multifaceted nature. Residual confounding cannot be excluded, particularly with respect to treatment-related variables and comorbid conditions.


## Conclusion

D2T RA accounted for 13.6% of this multicentre Saudi registry of patients with established RA. It was associated with persistent inflammatory activity, accumulated disease burden, functional disability, poorer patient-reported outcomes, and potentially modifiable patient-related factors, particularly treatment adherence. These findings support comprehensive assessment of both inflammatory and non-inflammatory contributors to difficult disease management. Longitudinal studies are required to clarify temporal and causal relationships and to inform individualized management strategies.
