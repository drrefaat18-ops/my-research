# Sitagliptin and tofacitinib ameliorate adjuvant induced arthritis via modulating the cross talk between JAK/STAT and TLR-4/NF-κB signaling pathways

**Authors:** Ibrahim SSA, Salama MA, Selima E, Shehata RR  
**Journal:** Life sciences (2020)  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.lfs.2020.118261  
**PDF:** pdf/Ibrahim2020.pdf

## Abstract

**Aims:** Rheumatoid arthritis is an autoimmune systemic disorder causing pain, swelling, stiffness, and disability in various joints. This work was designed to evaluate the effect of sitagliptin and tofacitinib on Janus kinase (JAK)/signaling transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) and toll like receptor (TLR-4)/nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) signaling pathways in adjuvant induced arthritis in rats.

**Materials and methods:** Severity of arthritis was evaluated and serum was analyzed for inflammatory mediators. The mRNA and protein expression level of the most important members of the two signaling pathways were determined. Lipid profile, transaminases and renal function parameters were assessed.

**Key findings:** Sitagliptin and tofacitinib significantly decreased the level of inflammatory parameters, the mRNA and protein expression level of the members of JAK/STAT and TLR-4/NF-κB pathways with more prominent effect of sitagliptin on TLR-4/NF-κB pathway and more expected obvious effect of tofacitinib on JAK/STAT pathway. The combination offered additional anti-inflammatory effect by inhibiting the cross talk between these pathways as inhibition of NF-κB activation decreased the serum level of IL-6 preventing the activation of STAT-3 in tibiotarsal tissues.

**Significance:** The combination of tofacitinib and sitagliptin normalized serum lipids and blood glucose level which could offer protection against cardiovascular diseases and caused partial reversal of serum transaminases and creatinine levels which can protect against tofacitinb's related hepato and nephrotoxicity. We could conclude that the combination of Sitagliptin with tofacitinib can offer synergistic anti-inflammatory effect and more protective action against side effects of tofacitinib.

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is an autoimmune disease that is characterized by infiltration of immune cells into the affected joints, release of inflammatory mediators, and subsequent joint damage (1). Cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6), IL-1 and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) are pivotal drivers of RA and other autoimmune diseases (2). Although biological agents targeting cytokines have revolutionized the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis, they are expensive, can lose effectiveness over time and cause minor to major adverse events (3). Therefore, development of new therapeutics continues to be the main target of recent research.

Among the recent approaches, are drugs targeting intracellular signal transduction pathways that are activated in RA, including the Janus kinase/signal transducers and activators of transcription (JAK/STAT) and toll like receptors/nuclear factor-kappa B (TLR/NF-kB) (4).

Janus kinases are a family of four tyrosine kinases (JAK1, JAK2, JAK3 and tyrosine kinase 2). Following cytokine ligation to JAK associated receptors, JAK phosphorylates the intracellular part of the receptor, which becomes activated, leading to the recruitment, phosphorylation and dimerization of STATs and their translocation into the nucleus, where they increase the expression of inflammatory cytokines (5). Pro-inflammatory cytokines produced by the synovial tissues, from arthritic joints, involve the activation of several Toll like receptors (6). Activation of Toll like receptor-4 (TLR-4) pathway, results in the translocation of NF-κB into the cell nucleus and the up-regulation of the expression of many pro-inflammatory cytokines (7). Moreover, there are cross regulations between STAT and NF-kB signaling pathways (8).

Sitagliptin, a DPP4 inhibitor, which is a class of anti-diabetic drugs has been reported to promote cardioprotection in type 2 diabetic rats by modulation of JAK/STAT pathway (9). Soluble DPP-4 has been shown to enhance TLR pathway and to augment inflammatory reactions that were ameliorated by DPP-4 inhibitors (10). However, its effect on signaling pathways in autoimmune diseases has not been investigated.

Tofacitinib is the first Jakinib developed for treatment of autoimmune diseases. It is a potent inhibitor of the Janus kinases (JAK1, JAK2 and JAK3), which are involved in the signaling of a large number of cytokines (11). Tofacitinib is effective for rheumatoid arthritis as a monotherapy or in combination with methotrexate and/or other non-biological disease modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (12). However, reported adverse effects of tofacitinib included infections, hypercholesterolemia, creatininemia and elevation of serum transaminases (13).

The aim of the present study was to investigate the modulating effect of sitagliptin and tofacitinib on JAK/STAT and TLR/NF-κB signaling pathways and the possible beneficial outcome of the combination was assessed.

2.2. Induction of Adjuvant arthritis (AA):

In this study, 5 groups were made each contain 10 rats. In groups 2-5, adjuvant arthritis was induced by injection of 0.1 ml suspension of heat-killed Mycobacterium butyricum (Difco Laboratories Co-USA), in incomplete Freund’s adjuvant (Sigma Aldrich Co-USA), intradermally at the base of the tail (14). Treatment was started on day 15, post-adjuvant injection, and was continued till day 24. Group 1 (healthy control rats) and group 2 (untreated arthritic rats) received the vehicles for tested drugs (methyl cellulose and water), orally daily. Arthritic rats in group 3 were treated orally with tofacitinib (Sigma, Aldrich, USA) dissolved in methyl cellulose at a dose of 6.2 mg/kg/day (15).

==Group 4 was treated orally with sitagliptin (MSD, USA) dissolved in distilled water at a dose of 100 mg/kg/day== ^ibrahim2020-2

(9) and group 5 received the combination of tofacitinib and sitagliptin in the previously mentioned doses.

Hind paw swelling was evaluated by measuring the width of the ankle joint of all rats, using vernier caliper, on day 0 then every other day till the end of the study.

The severity of arthritis was scored on 4 point scale, in which 0 = normal, 1 = slight edema of small digital joints, 2 = edema of the digital joints and footpad, 3 = gross edema of the entire footpad below the ankle or wrist, 4 = gross edema of the entire footpad including the ankle joint or wrist joint. The sum of the scores for all 4 limbs was calculated as the arthritic index. With a maximum score of 16 per rat (16). Arthrogram scores were assessed on day 14, 19 and 24.

On the 25th day, fasting blood glucose (FBG) was determined using automatic glucose meter (Accu-Check) (17), then animals were sacrificed by cervical dislocation. Blood samples were collected and serum was obtained and stored at -80 °C for determination of Anti-CCP, RANKL, TNF-α and IL-6 by rat enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (MyBiosource®,USA). All ELISA test kits were used according to the manufacturers' instructions.

Serum total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol, and triglyceride concentrations were measured using standard enzymatic methods with an automated analyzer (Hitachi 912, Hitachi Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). HDL cholesterol was measured after phosphotungstic acid and magnesium precipitation. LDL cholesterol concentration was calculated using the Friedwald formula (18).

The methods described by Reitman and Frankel were used to measure the serum levels of alanine transferase (ALT) and aspartate transferase (AST) (19). To estimate the serum levels of

renal injury markers, we used the methods described by Larsen for measurement of serum creatinine (20) and those described by Coulombe and Favreau for measurement of serum urea (21).

The synovial tissues of the tibiotarsal joints were removed (22) and stored at -80°C for determination of the mRNA expression of TLR-4, MYD-88, TRAF-6, NF-kB p65and IκB–α. Also the protein expression of total and phosphorylated STAT-3, PIAS-3, nuclear and cytoplasmic NF- kB p65, p- IκB -α, and IκB -α was measured by western blot technique.

Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic autoimmune systemic diseases causing disability in the population. The drugs used in this study were Tofacitinib and sitagliptin. Tofacitinib is a known JAK inhibitor already used in treatment of rheumatoid arthritis, however it has many side effects. Sitagliptin had been selected here in the present study as it was reported that it could promote cardioprotection in type 2 diabetic rats by modulation of JAK/STAT pathway (9). However, there

is a report suggesting that sitagliptin is associated with rheumatoid arthritis known to affect the shared epitope (SE) containing human leukocyte antigen (HLA)-DRB1 allele. In this case report the sitagliptin triggered RA in predisposing patients, this controversial result may be explained by the fact that diabetes mellitus has been associated with autoimmune disorders (25). This correlation seems to be related to the high levels of inflammatory mediators such as C-reactive protein (CRP), IL-6 and TNF-α, suggesting a role of diabetes in the immunepathogenesis of RA. Moreover, an alteration in the function of the immune system was observed in T2DM patient (26).

Severity of arthritis was evaluated based on changes in hind paw swelling and arthrogram scoring which are indicators of chronic systemic inflammation (27). There was a significant reduction in both parameters, with the most significant reduction in the combination treated group indicating a better anti-inflammatory effect. The pathogenesis of rheumatoid arthritis is likely to implicate Anti-CCP produced by activated B lymphocytes (28). Treatment with sitagliptin caused a significant decrease in the elevated Anti-CCP serum levels as previously documented in the same model of arthritis (29). The antiinflammatory and immunosuppressant effects of sitagliptin may be through inhibiting the dipeptidyl peptidase IV moiety of CD26 complex which cleaves proline rich proteins such as cartilage and joint proteins (30). A more significant decrease in serum Anti-CCP levels was demonstrated in the tofacitinib group probably because of its ability to decrease the synovial expression of IL-6 and IL-17 which are important regulators of Anti-CCP production (31,32). The decrease of Anti-CCP serum levels was more noticeable in the combination group. The interaction between activated lymphocytes and monocytes has been proposed as the mechanism leading to over production of the pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-6 from various immune cells (33). Also, excessive reactive oxygen species formation and depletion of antioxidants, in RA, can result in disturbance of the redox balance and activation of the signaling cascade NF-κB, which further exacerbates the production of cytokines (34). In the present work, sitagliptin effectively reduced serum IL-6 and TNF-α levels but to a lesser extent than tofacitinib. Reduced cardiac levels of IL-6 and JAK2 and STAT3 phosphorylation were also found in diabetic rats, treated with sitagliptin (9). Again, the favorable effect of the combination therapy was demonstrated in the lower serum IL-6 and TNF-α levels, than either drug alone. Simultaneous targeting of JAK/STAT pathway, involved in the production of a large number of the pro-inflammatory cytokines may account for such effect. In the current study, results of the histopathological examination of rats’ hind paws were in line with the results of other parameters of inflammation. Hind paws of normal rats revealed normal bone marrow, cartilage and joint space. In the untreated rats, examination of the hind paws showed cartilage degeneration and intense infilteration of inflammatory cells. Tofacitinib treated group showed showed mild inflammatory cell infiltration with normal cartilage, Sitagliptin treated group showed mild hyperplasia of synovial membrane and moderate inflammatory cell infiltration and the group treated with both drugs showed normal cartilage with no cellular infilteration.

Given the unique role of RANKL; an essential cytokine for osteoclastogenesis, in bone resorption, controlling osteoclast activity is crucial to prevent joint destruction in RA (15, 35).

Adjuvant induced arthritic rats showed a significant increase in serum RANKL levels as previously reported in collagen and adjuvant arthritis (35, 36). Sitagliptin caused a less significant decrease in serum RANKL levels compared to tofacitinib. Inhibition of phospholipase Cγdependent Ca+2 mobilization that induces osteoclasts upregulation, is a suggested mechanism for the anti-osteoclastogenic effect of sitagliptin (37). RANKL action on osteoclast precursors can be modulated by cytokines signaling through JAK-dependent pathways. Signaling via receptors for these cytokines was also potently inhibited by tofacitinib (38). JAK/STAT inhibition may also modulate fibroblast RANKL expression by decreasing IL-6 receptor signaling (39).

In the present work, all drugs used were able to decrease the expression of p-STAT-3, an effect that was paralleled by induction of PIAS3. PIAS3 binds to the STAT3 DNA-binding domains and thus prevents binding of STAT to target genes (40). In addition, TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway is known as pivotal links to inflammatory responses. Therefore, we established adjuvant induced arthritis in rats to explore the cascades of molecular events of both drugs inhibiting TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway. MyD88 is one of the main adaptor proteins for TLR4 to promote signal transduction. Thus, high mRNA level of MyD88 may indicate that the TLR4 pathway is activated. Meanwhile, MyD88- dependent pathway may activate NF-κB pathway via TRAF6 and IκBα intermediary molecules. In arthritic rats the mRNA levels of TLR4, MyD88, TRAF6, IĸBα and NF-kB p65 were significantly up-regulated. In treated groups the mRNA levels of these genes were all down-regulated with more reduction observed in sitagliptin treated group. These results suggested that adjuvant induced arthritis stimulates the generation of inflammatory reaction that promoted the combination of MyD88 with TRL4, then activated NF-κB, through recruiting TRAF6 and phosphorylating IĸBα. Both drugs exerted an anti-inflammatory and protective effects through inhibition of the combination of MyD88 with TRL4 and further suppressing the cascades activation of NF-κB signaling pathways (41).

To further validate the results of mRNA levels, the western blotting assay was carried out. At resting-state, NF-kB p65 is combing with the inhibitory protein IκB which can make NF-kB retained in the cytoplasm in an inactive forms of dimers. Once NF-kB signaling pathway is stimulated by external stimulus, concomitant IκB protein rapidly phosphorylated in response and then subsequently degraded by the proteosomal pathway. Finally, these reactions lead to the activation of NF-kB signaling pathway. And at the same time, NF-kB in cytoplasm is transferred into the nucleus. However, the IκBα only change in the cytoplasm. In our study there was a significant increase in the nuclear NF- kB p65 and cytoplasmic p IĸB levels in arthritic rats as compared to normal control groups. Both drugs were capable of causing significant reduction in the level of both parameters and the most significant inhibition was seen in the combination therapy indicating that the drugs used in the study were capable of inhibiting the NF- kB pathway (42).

Remarkably, we observed that sitagliptin and tofacitinib inhibited both JAK/STAT and TLR-4/NF-KB pathways with more prominent effect of sitagliptin on TLR-4/NF-KB pathway and more obvious expected effect of tofacitinib on JAK/STAT pathway. The offered synergistic antiinflammatory effect of the combination is mediated by modulating the crosstalk between the two pathways as by blocking NF-κB, caused reduction in the serum level of IL-6, diminishing the activation of STAT3 in tissues surrounding the hind paw. Considerable amount of data is available on the articular, as well as, the extra-articular (hematological, biochemical, metabolic) alterations, in the adjuvant arthritis model, allowing its

use to evaluate new possible therapeutic agents in the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis. In accordance with previous studies, results of the untreated adjuvant arthritis group demonstrated a significant elevation in several biochemical parameters including FBG, serum transaminases and serum urea and creatinine levels (43, 44) accompanied by a hypolipidemic state characterized by a significant decrease in total cholesterol, triglycerides, LDL and HDL serum levels (45,46). Sitagliptin alone and in combination with tofacitinib caused normalization of FBG, while values of FBG in the tofacitinib-treated group were still significantly higher than normal control. Besides sitagliptin’s mechanism of action as a DPP4 inhibitor, its reduction of proinflammatory cytokines; TNF-α and IL-6, involved in the abnormal glucose metabolism (44, 47), may play a role in its glucose lowering effect. The ability of sitagliptin to make a fall in serum lipid concentration would also potentially lead to an improvement in insulin sensitivity (48). However, sitagliptin alone caused a decrease in FBG without significantly altering circulating serum lipids according to the results of the present study. The hepatotoxic effect of tofacitinib, in the current work, was clearly evident from the significant elevation in serum ALT and AST, over untreated arthritic rats. The significant reduction in serum transaminases by sitagliptin, compared to tofacitinib, could be, in part, due to its enhancement of nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2, which is a transcriptional activator, serving as an oxidative stress sensor that increases superoxide dismutase activity leading to improvement of the liver antioxidant status (49). The combination of sitagliptin and tofacitinib effectively ameliorated the deleterious effect of tofacitinib by causing more significant reduction in serum transaminases.

Tofacitinib caused a small but significant increase in all lipid parameters, over normal rats, with the exception of HDL levels which were significantly elevated to near normal values. More increments in serum total cholesterol were reported, with higher doses of tofacitinib, with no significant effect on triglycerides levels (50).

Despite the possible role of JAK/STAT in cholesterol metabolism (46) and although the reduction of chronic systemic inflammation seems to be responsible for increasing serum circulating lipids (50), the significant anti-inflammatory and JAK inhibitory effects of sitagliptin, demonstrated in the present work, were not accompanied with significant changes in the lipid profile except for the significant increase in HDL levels. On the other hand, the combination of sitagliptin with tofacitinib, successfully normalized the elevated Total cholesterol, triglycerides and LDL levels, while significantly increasing HDL above normal values. Therefore, in addition to decreasing the inflammation, there may be specific mechanisms responsible for restoring the altered lipid balance (51). Sitagliptin alone, in the present work, significantly normalized creatinine and urea serum levels as previously reported with a lower dose of sitagliptin, resulting in prevention of diabetic nephropathy development (52). As hyperlipidemia, in concert with hyperglycemia, may be responsible for increased oxidative stress and initiation and aggravation of renal injury. Therefore, the beneficial effect of the combination therapy on FBG and serum lipids may be responsible for the improved renal function parameters, over tofacitinib alone, which supports the renoprotective effect of such combination.

## Conclusion

Results of the present study further support the modulatory effect of sitagliptin and tofacitinib on both JAK/STAT and TLR/NF-kB signaling pathways. The effect of sitagliptin was more

prominent on TLR/NF-kB signaling pathway. The aforementioned mechanisms are suggested to be the cause of offering synergistic anti-inflammatory effect of the combination therapy. Although the effect of sitagliptin treatment, on some of the biochemical alterations, in the adjuvant arthritis model, was variable, its favorable impact was quite evident, upon combination with tofacitinib, by normalization of blood glucose and serum lipids, which could offer protection against cardiovascular diseases, in rheumatoid arthritis patients. Moreover, by causing partial reversal of the elevated serum transaminases and serum creatinine levels, in the combination group, sitagliptin may also have a protective effect against tofacitinib’s related hepato and nephrotoxicity.
