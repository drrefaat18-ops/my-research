# The effects of fenofibrate on inflammation and cardiovascular markers in patients with active rheumatoid arthritis: a pilot study

**Authors:** Shirinsky Ivan, Polovnikova Oksana, Kalinovskaya Natalia, Shirinsky Valery  
**Journal:** Rheumatology International (2013)  
**DOI:** 10.1007/s00296-012-2613-z  
**PDF:** pdf/Shirinsky2013.pdf

## Abstract

Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors α (PPARα) agonists, or fibrates, are used in the treatment for dyslipidemia. Experimental data suggest that fibrates have anti-inflammatory properties, and PPARα is essential for the differentiation of endothelial progenitor cells (EPC) which diminished pool in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) contributes to accelerated atherosclerosis. The data on fibrates' effects in patients with RA are limited. The aim of this study was to investigate changes in disease activity, inflammatory markers, lipid profile, and circulating EPC in active RA patients treated with fenofibrate.

==Twenty-seven patients with active RA taking traditional disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) were prescribed fenofibrate (145 mg/day) for 3 months.== ^shirinsky2013-1

All patients received background traditional DMARDs in stable doses. The outcomes measured were clinical disease activity variables, circulating cytokines, adipokines, lipids, and EPC. Twenty-five patients completed the study. At the end of treatment, there was a significant reduction in DAS28, all individual DAS28 components except tender joint count, the duration of morning stiffness, and in the patient's assessment of disease activity. Fifteen (60 %) patients achieved good/moderate EULAR response, while 10 (40 %) patients satisfied ACR20 response criteria. Treatment with fenofibrate resulted in significant decrease in CRP and IL-6 concentrations and improvement in lipid profile. There was no change in the concentrations of circulating EPC. In conclusion, fenofibrate treatment is associated with reduced inflammation and improved lipid profile in RA patients. Large randomized controlled studies are needed to confirm these results and to define the role of fibrates in the treatment for RA.

**Keywords:** Arthritis · Rheumatoid · PPAR alpha · Fenofibrate

## Introduction

Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs) are members of steroid hormone receptor superfamily, acting at the crossroads of pathways of lipid metabolism, glucose homeostasis, and inflammatory processes. There are three isotypes of PPAR (α, β/δ, and γ) which have distinct but overlapping functions. PPARα has been proposed as a key lipid metabolism modulator and regulator of inflammation [1]. Fibrates, which are specific pharmacological agonists of PPARα, have been widely used in the treatment for hypercholesterolemia and hypertriglyceridemia. Apart from their metabolic action, anti-inflammatory properties of fibrates have been described, including inhibition of NF-kappa B signaling and pro-inflammatory cytokine production by rheumatoid arthritis (RA) synovial fibroblasts [2]. In rats, fenofibrate treatment leads to decrease in severity and progression of adjuvant-induced arthritis [2]. A recent study suggests that PPARα is involved in the differentiation of endothelial progenitor cells (EPC) [3] which diminished pool in RA is thought to contribute to enhanced atherosclerosis RA [4]. Based on these data, fibrates may have a role in reducing inflammation and atherosclerosis in human autoimmune diseases including RA.

Therefore, we performed a pilot study evaluating safety, anti-inflammatory, and lipid-lowering effects of fenofibrate in RA patients taking traditional disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs). We also studied the effect of fenofibrate treatment on the concentrations of circulating EPC.

## Materials and methods

The study was approved by the local ethics committee of our institution and all patients provided written informed consent. Patients selected for inclusion had been at least 18 year old with active RA defined as DAS28 ≥3.2. Patients were allowed to continue DMARDs and prednisolone (<10 mg/day) in stable doses for at least 2 months prior to the study and during the study period. Patients were prescribed fenofibrate 145 mg/day for 12 weeks. The evaluations of all clinical measures and ESR were made at baseline and every 4 weeks thereafter. Other laboratory parameters were assessed at baseline and after 12 weeks of treatment. Clinical efficacy endpoints included tender and swollen joint counts, patient's and physician's assessment of disease activity, patient's assessment of general health and pain level measured by visual analog scale (VAS), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), and health assessment questionnaire (HAQ). Disease activity was evaluated using DAS28 index. Clinical response to treatment was assessed using EULAR and ACR criteria. Laboratory parameters studied were concentrations of circulating CRP, total cholesterol (TC), high density cholesterol (HDL-C), triglycerides, IL-6, IL-17, IL-1β, IL-10, IFN-γ, resistin, adiponectin, and EPC. Serum cytokine and adipokine levels were measured using commercial ELISAs; EPC frequencies were determined by flow cytometry as CD34+/CD144+/CD3− cells within the lymphocyte gate as described elsewhere [5]. Safety was assessed by patients' reports of adverse events and clinical examination. Sixteen patients with erosive osteoarthritis were used as controls for baseline EPC measurements.

Statistical analysis was made using Friedman test for repeated measures (all clinical measures and ESR) and Wilcoxon signed-rank test for before/after treatment comparisons (other laboratory parameters). Descriptive statistics is presented as median (95 % confidence interval, CI). Adjustments for multiple comparisons were not performed due to the exploratory nature of this study and small group size.

## Results

Twenty-seven patients (24 women, three men) were included in the study. All patients had active disease (median DAS28 6.29, 95 % CI 6.15–6.65). Median age of patients was 59.7 years (95 % CI 55.51–62.99) with median disease duration of 9.6 years (95 % CI 8.27–18.48).

==At the end of treatment, there was a significant reduction in DAS28 score from a median 6.28 (95 % CI 6.03–6.59) to 4.83 (95 % CI 4.3–5.37).== ^shirinsky2013-3

Significant improvements were observed in all individual DAS28 variables except number of tender joints (Fig. 1a). Among other clinical parameters tested, statistically significant declines were found in the patients' assessment of disease activity and the duration of morning stiffness (Fig. 1b). At week 12 of the treatment, 15 (60 %) patients achieved good/moderate EULAR response. Ten (40 %), four (16 %), and one (4 %) patients developed ACR20, ACR50, and ACR70 responses, respectively.

==Treatment with fenofibrate was associated with a significant decrease in serum CRP and IL-6 concentrations by 35.71 and 41 %, respectively (Table 1).== ^shirinsky2013-4

The concentrations of other cytokines and adipokines did not change (data not shown).

Fenofibrate treatment also resulted in decreased TC, triglycerides levels, and elevated HDL-C concentrations (Table 1).

At baseline, the concentrations of EPC were lower in RA than in 15 control OA patients (0.17 and 0.3 %, respectively; p = 0.036). After 12 weeks of treatment, no significant changes were observed in circulating EPC levels (Table 1).

==The treatment was well tolerated, no serious adverse events occurred.== ^shirinsky2013-7

Two patients developed acute low back pain, one patient had transient (5 days) self-limited nausea, and another patient had short-term (lasting 1 day) mild diarrhea.

## Discussion

In this study, we observed improvement in parameters of disease activity, systemic inflammation, and lipid metabolism in active RA patients treated with fenofibrate.

There are abundant experimental data demonstrating fibrates' anti-inflammatory effects both in vitro and in various disease models. The main molecular mechanism by which fibrates suppress inflammation seems to be the inhibition of nuclear factor-kappa B (NFκB) [6]. It is of note that commonly used DMARD, sulfasalazine, is thought to exert its action via down-regulation of NFκB activity [7].

The first evidence of fibrates anti-inflammatory activity in humans comes mostly from patients with hyperlipidemia [8] and metabolic syndrome [9]. Fenofibrate treatment had been associated with decreased concentrations of CRP and IL-6 which we have also observed in our study. In the only reported study on fibrates in RA to date, Goto used different set of clinical efficacy and disease activity measures (prednisolone usage, CRP levels, and ESR) [10]. Of the above parameters, only prednisolone dosage significantly decreased after 6 months of fenofibrate treatment [10].

In a PPARα knockout model, it was shown that PPARα is essential for microparticle-induced differentiation of EPC [3]. The inability of fenofibrate to restore the circulating EPC pool in our study may suggest that fibrates do not impact this cell population in vivo. Another possibility is that PPARα activation primarily influences EPC functional properties which were not assessed in this study.

An effect of fenofibrate on HDL-C concentrations revealed in our study is of clinical relevance as RA patients are characterized by decreased HDL-C [11] which is one of the strongest independent risk factors for cardiovascular disease [12]. Fenofibrate's cholesterol and triglyceride lowering effects may also have clinical implications in RA patients in whom both triglyceride and TC levels had been shown to be significant predictors of ischemic stroke [13].

In spite of small number of patients and non-controlled open-label design, our study suggests that fenofibrate could have clinical utility for RA. Large-scale, controlled studies are needed to confirm ability of fenofibrate to control clinical manifestations of RA and to reduce cardiovascular morbidity in this group of patients. Moreover, elucidating the molecular mechanisms that underlie fibrates anti-inflammatory action in RA may provide a new understanding of the link between inflammation and lipid metabolism which can lead to the development of novel therapeutic targets.
