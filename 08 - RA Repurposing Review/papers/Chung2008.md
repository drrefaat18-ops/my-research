# Inflammation-Associated Insulin Resistance: Differential Effects in Rheumatoid Arthritis and Systemic Lupus Erythematosus Define Potential Mechanisms

**Authors:** Chung Cecilia P., Oeser Annette, Solus Joseph F., Gebretsadik Tebeb, Shintani Ayumi, Avalos Ingrid, Sokka Tuulikki, Raggi Paolo, Pincus Theodore, Stein C. Michael  
**Journal:** Arthritis and rheumatism (2008)  
**DOI:** 10.1002/art.23600  
**PDF:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2755593/


## Abstract


#### Objective

Insulin resistance is increased by inflammation, but the mechanisms are unclear. The present study was undertaken to test the hypothesis that decreased insulin sensitivity is differentially associated with mediators of inflammation by studying 2 chronic inflammatory diseases of different pathogenesis, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and rheumatoid arthritis (RA).


#### Methods

We measured fasting insulin, glucose, and lipid levels, erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP), interleukin-6 (IL-6), tumor necrosis factor α (TNFα), and coronary artery calcification in 103 patients with SLE and in 124 patients with RA. Insulin sensitivity was measured using the homeostasis model assessment (HOMA) index.


#### Results

The HOMA value was higher in RA patients (median 2.05 [interquartile range (IQR) 1.05–3.54]) than in SLE patients (1.40 [0.78–2.59]) (P = 0.007). CRP and ESR did not differ significantly in RA and SLE patients. Body mass index (BMI) was significantly correlated with the HOMA index in both RA (ρ = 0.20) and SLE (ρ = 0.54), independently of age, sex, race, and current use of corticosteroids.

In SLE patients, the HOMA index was also significantly correlated with ESR (ρ = 0.35) and CRP (ρ = 0.25), but not with other variables. The association between the ESR and the HOMA value in patients with SLE remained significant after adjustment for confounding covariates (P = 0.008). In multivariable models, the major contributing factors to the HOMA index were the BMI in SLE patients, and IL-6 and TNFα levels in RA patients.


#### Conclusion

The pathogenesis of insulin resistance and its contribution to atherogenesis varies in different inflammatory settings.
