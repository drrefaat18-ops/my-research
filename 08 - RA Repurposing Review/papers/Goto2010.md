# A comparative study of anti-inflammatory and antidyslipidemic effects of fenofibrate and statins on rheumatoid arthritis

**Authors:** Goto Makoto  
**Journal:** Modern Rheumatology (2010)  
**DOI:** 10.1007/s10165-009-0261-2  
**PDF:** pdf/Goto2010.pdf

## Abstract

We prospectively compared the anti-inflammatory and antidyslipidemic effects of fenofibrate and statins in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) patients. Forty-four RA patients [male (M) = 7, female (F) = 37] with dyslipidemia were enrolled in this 6-month study and randomly allocated to the fenofibrate (2 M + 21 F = 23) or statins (5 M + 16 F = 21) group. We measured blood chemistry (serum lipid profile, sugar, urate, and γ-glutamyl transpeptidase) and blood pressure 2 h after breakfast. Visual analog scale (VAS), C-reactive protein (CRP), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), and prednisolone (PSL) dosage were also recorded immediately before and after the study. Fenofibrate, but not statins, significantly decreased serum levels of total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein–cholesterol, and triglycerides (all p < 0.05). A significant improvement in VAS was observed in both the fenofibrate group (49.1 ± 24.7 → 14.7 ± 11.2; p < 0.0001) and the statins group (47.4 ± 29.7 → 20.2 ± 16.5; p < 0.001). PSL dosage significantly decreased only in the fenofibrate group (3.58 ± 2.68 → 2.00 ± 2.22 mg/day; p < 0.01). Significant correlation was observed between ΔVAS and ΔCRP in the fenofibrate group (p < 0.05). Fenofibrate showed more anti-inflammatory and antidyslipidemic activity than statins in RA.

**Keywords:** Clinical trial · Dyslipidaemia · Fenofibrate · Rheumatoid arthritis · Statin

## Introduction

The major causes of death in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) include infections of various kinds that are associated with immunological abnormalities and immunosuppressive therapy inherent to the disease. These are followed by cardiac/vascular diseases associated with atherosclerosis, such as myocardial and cerebral infarctions [1, 2]. Dyslipidemia, a risk factor for atherosclerosis, has been shown to be an adverse event of steroid therapy in RA patients [3].

The Trial of Atorvastatin in Rheumatoid Arthritis (TARA Study), comparing atorvastatin, a lipid-lowering statin, to a placebo, was conducted by McCarey et al. in 2004 [4]. Six-month administration of atorvastatin significantly improved the Disease Activity Score 28 (DAS28), C-reactive protein (CRP), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), and number of swollen joints. The anti-inflammatory effects of atorvastatin thus received special attention. A report in Nature also details the clinical anti-inflammatory activity of fenofibrate, a lipid-lowering fibrate agonist of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-α (PPAR-α) [5]. We thus anticipated that lipid-lowering agents with pleiotropic effects may serve as anti-inflammatory reagents in RA.

We administered lipid-lowering agents with different mechanisms of action (fenofibrate, statins) to patients with RA complicated by dyslipidemia to study their beneficial effects on serum lipid levels and their anti-inflammatory activity.

## Materials and methods

==Forty-four patients with RA complicated by dyslipidemia were randomly assigned to either the fenofibrate group (Lipidil, Kaken Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., 200 mg/day, n = 23) or to the statins group (n = 21).== ^goto2010-1

Diagnosis of dyslipidemia was made if one of the following criteria was met: total cholesterol (TC) ≥ 220 mg/dl, low-density lipoprotein–cholesterol (LDL–C) ≥ 140 mg/dl, high-density lipoprotein–cholesterol (HDL–C) < 40 mg/dl, or triglycerides (TG) ≥ 150 mg/dl. The baseline blood chemistry (serum lipids profile, sugar, uric acid, and liver function) and blood pressure were compared with those obtained 6 months after the study. The effects on RA were assessed by comparing the visual analog scale (VAS) by patient assessment, CRP, ESR, rheumatoid factor (RF), and dosages of prednisolone (PSL). A patient informed consent form, which had been formally approved by the Ethical Committee of Toin University of Yokohama, was used to explain the purpose of this study and the drugs to be administered. The numerical results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). For statistical analysis, Fisher's exact test was used to assess patients' background, Wilcoxon signed rank test for comparison between baseline and posttreatment data, Mann–Whitney U test for intergroup comparison, and Spearman rank correlation coefficient for ΔVAS. P < 0.05 was regarded as significant.

## Results

### Patients' background

Patient data are shown in Table 1 and Fig. 1. Mean age was 63.5 ± 10.3 years in the fenofibrate group (M = 2, F = 21) and 65.7 ± 10.0 years in the statins group (M = 5, F = 16). There were no significant differences in age or sex between the 2 groups. Although baseline TC and LDL–C levels were significantly higher in the fenofibrate group (p < 0.05 and p < 0.01, respectively), no significant differences were noted in other lipid profiles between the 2 groups. Among disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) users, methotrexate (MTX) was prescribed to 3 of 15 patients in the fenofibrate group and 2 of 12 in the statins group. No statistically significant difference (p = 0.3720 by Fisher's exact test) was observed between groups concerning MTX usage. No patients received biologics, and no episode was recorded regarding changes or addition of other DMARDs during the study.

### Improvement in serum lipid levels

TC, LDL–C, and TG levels decreased significantly after the study (p = 0.0094, p = 0.0154, and p = 0.0111, respectively) in the fenofibrate group but not in the statins group (Fig. 1). HDL–C levels in both groups, relatively high before the study, did not change significantly (Table 2).

### Improvement in inflammatory activity

The VAS showed significant improvements for both groups: from 49.1 ± 24.7 to 14.7 ± 11.2 mm in the fenofibrate group (p < 0.0001), and from 47.4 ± 29.7 to 20.2 ± 16.5 mm in the statins group (p = 0.0007) (Fig. 2).

==The dosage of PSL significantly decreased only in the fenofibrate group: from 3.6 ± 2.7 to 2.0 ± 2.2 mg/day (p = 0.0098), and there was a significant difference of PSL dosage after the study between fenofibrate group and statins group (p = 0.0449) (Fig. 3).== ^goto2010-2

The ΔVAS value (difference between baseline and posttreatment VAS measurements) was significantly correlated with ΔCRP values (differences between baseline and posttreatment CRP) (p = 0.0207) in the fenofibrate group (Table 3).

==Although the CRP level decreased from 1.0 ± 2.1 to 0.7 ± 1.2 mg/dl in the fenofibrate group and from 1.1 ± 1.4 to 0.7 ± 1.3 mg/dl in the statins group, the changes were not statistically significant.== ^goto2010-3

No significant changes in ESR (p = 0.7536 in the fenofibrate group, p = 0.2370 in the statins group by Wilcoxon signed rank test) or RF between pre- and poststudy were observed in either group (Table 2). A significantly negative correlation was observed between ΔVAS and ΔHDL–C in the statins group (r = -0.525, p = 0.0175).

### Changes in other body parameters

After the study, HbA1c level improved significantly in the fenofibrate group (p < 0.05). However, no significant changes were seen in body weight, waist circumference, blood pressure, γ-GTP, serum uric acid, or blood sugar levels in either group. No adverse drug reactions, including abnormal laboratory findings, were observed. In addition, no significant differences were observed in other body parameters between the intergroup comparison (Table 2). Only 4 of the 44 RA patients (9.1%) met the criteria for metabolic syndrome based on measurements for waist circumference, blood pressure, lipid profile, and sugar metabolism [6].

## Discussion

The average lifespan of RA patients is 8–10 years shorter than normal. Hakoda et al. reported in 2005 the 1.6 times higher mortality rate in RA patients than the general population, based on a survey of atomic bomb survivors in Hiroshima [7]. The pathological conditions that increase the mortality risk included liver disease, tuberculosis, pneumonia, and respiratory disease. Shinomiya et al. indicated, contrary to the previous pattern of respiratory disease being the major cause of death in RA patients, that atherosclerotic lesions such as coronary vascular diseases and cerebrovascular diseases have, in recent years, become a more common cause of death [2]. Dyslipidemia is known to be one of the risk factors for atherosclerosis in general, as well as a major adverse drug event of steroids in RA sufferers [3]. As yet, few trials have been reported on the use of lipid-lowering agents to assess antirheumatic activity in RA patients.

Kanda et al. administered a statin (simvastatin, 10 mg) for 3 months to 8 patients with RA complicated with dyslipidemia and reported an improvement in ESR and CRP levels. They used the American College of Rheumatology 20% improvement criterion (ACR20) as their index of outcome and reported an achievement ratio of 80% [8]. In the relatively large-scale TARA Study, 116 RA patients were given either atorvastatin (40 mg/day) or a placebo for 6 months. The results showed a more significant improvement in DAS28, CRP level, ESR, and the number of swollen joints in the atorvastatin group than in the placebo group. Thus, the anti-inflammatory effect of atorvastatin has so far received greater attention [4].

Of the fibrates, only fenofibrate has been sporadically examined for its anti-inflammatory activity. Okamoto et al. reported improvements in inflammatory markers, subjective symptoms, Health Assessment Questionnaire (HAQ) score, and number of painful joints in a 33-year-old female RA patient who was administered fenofibrate [9]. Murai et al. administered fenofibrate to 5 RA patients for 6 months and observed decreases in CRP and serum amyloid A protein (SAA) after 2 months. They also reported the successful reduction of MTX and PSL dosages in one case [10].

In the past, we have been able to reduce the use of steroids and DMARDs during the administration of statins and fenofibrate to RA patients. These observations prompted us to make a comparison between the anti-inflammatory effects of statins and fenofibrate.

Inhibition of nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) has been proposed as having a possible anti-inflammatory mechanism. NF-κB forms a complex with an inactivator, called IκB, and acts as a complex form in the cytoplasm. Proinflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-1 (IL-1) degrade IκB, and NF-κB migrates from the cytoplasm into the nucleus. This appears to be how the target gene involved in inflammation is activated. PPAR-α ligands, including fenofibrate, have been shown to induce IκB expression, leading to the inhibition of NF-κB signal transduction [11].

Okamoto et al. reported the expression of PPAR-α in RA synovial fibroblasts and confirmed that fenofibrate, but not bezafibrate, inhibited proinflammatory cytokine production, NF-κB nuclear entry, and differentiation of osteoclastic precursors into osteoclasts. They also reported the protective activity of fenofibrate in rat arthritis models [12]. Among a series of statins, simvastatin has been reported to regulate proinflammatory cytokine production and NF-κB activity in fibroblast-like synoviocytes from RA patients [13].

The Justification for the Use of Statins in Primary Prevention: An Intervention Trial Evaluating Rosuvastatin (JUPITER Study), published in 2008 [14], indicated more significant inhibition of cardiovascular events in patients treated with rosuvastatin at a dose of 20 mg/day than in placebo-treated patients. The study was evaluated in approximately 18,000 patients whose LDL–C levels were normal but whose CRP levels > 0.2 mg/dl. The median LDL–C and CRP levels after 12 months of rosuvastatin treatment remained at low levels. These results reconfirmed the anti-inflammatory activity of rosuvastatin. However, the administration of rosuvastatin significantly increased the incidence of newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes mellitus.

The Fenofibrate Intervention and Event Lowering in Diabetes (FIELD Study), published in 2005, reported the inhibitory effect of fenofibrate on cardiovascular events in type 2 diabetes patients by reducing blood glucose levels [15]. In our study of RA patients, a significant decrease in HbA1c was observed only in the fenofibrate group. Oxidative stress in association with inflammation has been shown to increase under hyperglycemic conditions. Fenofibrate was reported to improve both fasting and postprandial lipoproteinemia, oxidative stress, and inflammation in metabolic syndrome patients with high TG [16].

As the regular dosage of fenofibrate ameliorated inflammation irrespective of improvement in dyslipidemia, the anti-inflammatory action of fenofibrate may work indirectly with antidyslipidemic activity. In contrast, as the maximum dosages of statins showed modest improvement of inflammation in association with improvement of ΔHDL–C, both anti-inflammatory and antidyslipidemic activities of statins may function by using the similar pathway.

Chronic inflammation is a focus of keen research interest, as it may be a basic driving force behind not only aging-associated diseases but also natural aging itself; and a concept termed inflammaging (inflammation + aging) has been proposed. RA is regarded as a disorder that typically promotes such inflammaging [17].

This study demonstrates the significant benefits of fenofibrate usage for the treatment of RA accompanied by dyslipidemia.
