Introduction 
Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic autoimmune disease char acterized by persistent 
synovial inflammation and joint destruction, driven by a complex network of pro-inflammatory 
cytokines and intracellular signaling cascades like NF -kB and MAPK. Glucocorticoids are 
highly effective for RA management but are associated with severe long-term adverse effects, 
creating a need for glucocorticoid-sparing agents. Recent studies into various natural products 
have identified compounds which may potentiate the anti -inflammatory effects of low -dose 
glucocorticoids via PPAR-alpha and glucocorticoid receptor-alpha crosstalk. 
Aim of the work 
This study aims to investigate the potential of selected natural products as a glucocorticoid-
sparing agent, and to evaluate the efficacy of this combination against established 
antirheumatic agents in adjuvant-induced arthritis. 
Study Design 
This study employs the Complete Freund’s Adjuvant (CFA) -induced arthritis model in 
female Wistar rats to investigate the glucocorticoid -sparing potential of micronized 
palmitoylethanolamide (m-PEA), both alone and in combination with prednisolone, relative to 
the reference agents baricitinib and tacrolimus. Arthritis is induced on Day 0 by a single 
subcutaneous injection of 0.3 mL CFA into the right inguinal/proximal thigh region, while 
negative control animals receive an equivalent subcutaneous injection of 0.25 mL paraffin oil. 
Following a 15 -day induction period, oral treatment is initiated on Day 16 and administered 
once daily until Day 45, after which all animals are sacrificed on Day 46  for terminal sample 
collection. 
Animals are allocated to nine experimental groups [number of rats per group not specified]: 
a negative control group, a positive (CFA arthritic) control group, a prednisolone monotherapy 
group (10 mg/kg), an m -PEA monothera py group (200 mg/kg), three PEA/prednisolone 
combination groups at ascending PEA doses (50/5, 100/5, and 200/5 mg/kg), a tacrolimus 
reference group (1.8 mg/kg), and a baricitinib reference group (10 mg/kg). This design permits 
evaluation of whether escalat ing doses of m -PEA in combination with a fixed low dose of 
prednisolone can achieve comparable therapeutic efficacy to full -dose prednisolone 
monotherapy or to the established reference agents, thereby addressing the study’s 
glucocorticoid-sparing objective.

Measured Parameters 
Disease activity and underlying molecular mechanisms are assessed using serum and knee 
joint tissue samples collected at sacrifice. Serum samples are analyzed by ELISA for anti -
citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPA) and the pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α, IL-1β, and 
IL-6. Knee joints are proces sed for histopathological examination to assess structural joint 
changes, and for immunohistochemical evaluation of NF-κB p65 nuclear expression and TLR4 
expression as markers of inflammatory signaling activation. Additionally, knee joint tissue 
homogenates are analyzed by ELISA for p38 MAPK expression to further characterize the 
intracellular inflammatory signaling pathways involved.
