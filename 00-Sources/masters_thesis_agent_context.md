## 2. Main Research Idea

The master's project studies the potential role of **micronized palmitoylethanolamide (m-PEA)** in experimental rheumatoid arthritis.

The project currently has two main scientific directions:

1. Evaluate whether **m-PEA can act as a glucocorticoid-sparing agent**, allowing lower-dose prednisolone while maintaining or improving anti-inflammatory/anti-arthritic efficacy.
2. Compare the **m-PEA + prednisolone combinations** with other pharmacological treatments, particularly:
   - **Baricitinib**
   - **Tacrolimus**

The exact official thesis title and final one-sentence objective may still be refined, so do not invent a final title unless it exists in the source files.

## 8. Scientific Comparison Logic

The project is not simply testing whether PEA is anti-inflammatory.

The stronger research question is:

> Can m-PEA improve arthritis control while allowing a lower prednisolone dose, and how does this strategy compare with established/modern immunomodulatory treatments such as tacrolimus and baricitinib?

This **steroid-sparing concept** is central to understanding the project.

## 13. One-Paragraph Project Summary

This master's project investigates **micronized palmitoylethanolamide (m-PEA)** in a **CFA-induced arthritis model in female Wistar rats**, with particular focus on its potential use as a **glucocorticoid-sparing adjunct to prednisolone**. The study includes m-PEA alone, prednisolone alone, multiple m-PEA/prednisolone dose combinations, and comparator treatments including **tacrolimus and baricitinib**. Outcomes focus on inflammatory and rheumatoid-arthritis-related biomarkers, knee-joint pathology, and signaling pathways such as **NF-κB and p38 MAPK**, with TLR4 also considered in the project plan. The key scientific question is whether combining m-PEA with a reduced prednisolone dose can provide meaningful anti-arthritic activity while reducing reliance on higher glucocorticoid exposure.
